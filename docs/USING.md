# Using Polku

[Open Polku](https://polku.si) · [Overview](../README.md)

## Plan and edit a route

Open **Plan**, choose a sport and add points on the map. Drag a point or the route line to change the plan. On a phone, hold the line before dragging it.

With no route yet, the planner offers four ways to begin under **Start a route** and your sport: **Draw it**, **Round trip**, **Marked routes** and **A file**. The sport there is the same as the button beside the search. A tap or click on the map starts drawing at once. Each way has **‹ Ways to begin** at its top left, which brings the four back; on a phone the back gesture does the same. The sport is in a row of its own under the top row, in **Start a route** and in each way; **✕** at the top right closes the planner.

- **Draw it** says how Polku joins your points in the drawing mode you've chosen, and lets you pick the route style before the first leg; **Fine-tune** opens How you go's controls there. A style picked before there's a route routes nothing.
- **Round trip:** from the ways to begin, it first asks for its start: tap the map, or use **Where I am**, which sends your position to the router as the start, rounded to about a metre. With a start, the rest of the form appears: **How long**, by **Distance** or **Time**, with the sport's lengths (three on a phone, four on a desktop) or **Other…** for your own (typed, or **−** and **+**); then the route style and the direction. Then **Make 3 loops**, which says what it costs in routing calls. After your first point, **Round trip from here** offers three lengths and **Other…**. While the loops are made the map waits. Tap a loop's card or its line on the map to pick it, and **Use loop A** to take it; **Undo** takes it back.
- **Marked routes** lists LIPAS's routes for your sport in the map's view. Tap a card or a route's line to pick it, and **Follow this route** to make it your route on its own line. It says when the map doesn't show them (**Show them**), when LIPAS isn't answering (**Retry**) and when there are none in view. **Find by name** searches all of Finland.

The planner's header shows your route: its distance, time and climb. How the next leg is drawn is in the toolbox, with undo, redo and **More** (⋯). The toolbox grows with the route: before the first point it has only undo and redo, and only when there's something to undo; from the first point, the drawing mode and **More**; once there's a leg, the route's actions too. While a round trip is open it has only undo and redo, until you take a loop or close it.

- **On a phone** the toolbox floats above the planner, beside the map's buttons. Where there's no room for it there (a narrow phone, large text, the planner pulled up), it's the planner's first row. The drawing button shows the mode, and under it the next leg's sport and route style ("Hike · Balanced"). Tap it to draw the next legs with **Follow paths**, **Straight line** or **Over water**, to pick their sport and see your speed in it, or to pick a route style for the whole route. **Done** is at the right of the route's numbers.
- **On a desktop** it's a toolbar at the bottom of the planner's edge, with the map's scale and credits just above it: the three modes, the next leg's sport, the route style, undo and redo, **Show the whole route**, the route's actions that fit, **More** and **Done**. Point at a button, or reach it with the keyboard, for its name, its key and what it does. In a narrow window, more of them move into More, the sport shows only its icon, and in the narrowest the toolbar takes a second row. Moving the planner between the side and the bottom frames your route again.

**Done** ends editing and keeps your route open to read, check and take along: the route's view. Above its numbers are the route's name, its sports and **Kept on this device**, with **✕** at the top right. The drawing tools go and **More** stays; on a desktop the toolbar also keeps **Show the whole route**. A tap on the map then shows what's there instead of adding a point, a tap on your route opens its card, and the points stay where they are. **Route** has the same sections, without **Reorder**. **Edit**, where **Done** was, goes back to drawing, with undo as it was. **Route from here**, **Add to route**, a round trip, a file and a marked route to follow go back to editing on their own. With only your first point, **Done** closes the planner instead. A shared route opens in the route's view.

**✕** closes the planner. Your route stays on the map, and a note says so, with **Open**. **Plan** opens the planner again as you left it: in the route's view or editing, at the size you left it. After a reload it opens again if it was open.

A mode changes only the legs you draw next. To change a drawn leg, select it on the map. A route style is for the whole route and routes it again; a leg with a style of its own keeps it. The route's actions are a share link, GPX export and saving offline; reversing it and a leg back to the start; and **Clear route**, which a note lets you undo. Those that fit are on the toolbox or toolbar, and **More** has the rest, with a round trip from the start and importing a GPX or KML file. **Show the whole route** is under **Locate** with the map's buttons whenever a route is on the map (on a desktop, while the planner is closed).

### Sports along a route

A route can change sport from one leg to the next: ride to the shore, paddle across, then run. **Draw it** says so before the first point.

- **The next legs:** pick their sport in the drawing menu (on a desktop, the sport button on the toolbar). What you've drawn stays as it is, the map switches to that sport, and Paddle and Swim draw over water. Where the sport changes, the time includes a changeover. Under the sport is your speed in it; **Change** opens your speeds right there, and a change re-times the route.
- **The button beside the search** sets the map's sport. With a route in another sport, it asks what the new sport is for: **The whole route** plans the route's legs again in it, says how many routing calls that takes, and **Undo** brings everything back; **Only the legs you draw next** keeps the route as it is; **Keep** changes nothing. A route on land can't become a water route that way: change a single leg from its menu on the map instead.
- **A point on open water:** while you draw on land, Polku asks how you get there: **Paddle**, **Swim** or **Stay on land**, and in winter over the ice too. Paddle and Swim take the leg to the point over the water and keep the point where you tapped it; the legs after it carry on in that sport. A leg over the ice is a straight line, and Check says that Polku can't see whether the ice holds: check the ice yourself before you go. A point on a bridge or a ferry line, or right by the shore, doesn't ask.
- **The map's sport** doesn't change your route: the route keeps its sports and its own route style for each of them.
- **Your speeds** for every sport are also in **Settings → Your speeds**, one line each; tap a sport to change them. Polku times your route with them.
- **Your craft** for paddling (**Sea kayak**, **Canoe** or **SUP**) is in the sport menus when the sport is Paddle, and in your speeds. Polku keeps a canoe or a SUP to calmer water than a sea kayak.

While you plan, **Weather** and <kbd>T</kbd> open the weather timeline over the map, and the planner stays where it was. On a phone the timeline sits just above the planner, with the toolbox and the map's buttons above it; with the planner pulled up to half or more, it sits on the planner's top until you close it. Go keeps the departure and the weather along the route.

Tap or click a point for its card: its name, when you'd get there, how far it is from the start and its height. **At this point** switches between **Pass by**, **Stop** (its minutes with − and +) and **Night** (**Stay**, the nights with − and +; **Go on at**, which opens your phone's clock, its line the day you go on; and **Places to sleep nearby**). The start's card has **Back to start** first while you draw, and **Leave** (below). **Sport from here** changes the legs from the point to the next change of sport, or the end; **Paddle** has your craft under it, for all your paddle legs, and a change of sport has its changeover minutes and **Take the bike along**. **Next stretch, to …** opens that stretch's card. **Move** asks you to tap where the point goes (**Cancel** or **Escape** leaves it), **Rename** types its name where it's shown, **Start here** and **End here** say what they remove, the start and the end have **Reverse**, the end **Back to start**, and **Remove** is at the bottom, with Undo in its note. On a phone the card is in the planner and only undo and redo stay beside it; on a desktop it opens at the point, its edits are buttons, and the point's row in the list lights up. **✕**, **Escape**, the back gesture or a tap on the map closes it, the planner as it was, and that tap adds no point. In the route's view the card has the stop or night and the leaving time, with **Edit**.

Tap or click the route for its card: the way there, three numbers for your sport (when you'd be there, how steep, and the difficulty, the trail grade or the surface; on the water the wind and temperature) and the stretch's most serious warning. **Add a point here** is right at the spot: on a phone a pill just above it, on a desktop the card's first button. **This stretch** runs to the next point you see, over the points that only shape the line, with its numbers and what to look out for; **Edit this stretch** sets its sport, how it's drawn and its style in one step of **Undo**, and has **Other ways**, **Stop here**, **Avoid this spot** and **On the profile**. **What's here** lists what the map knows of the way, your sport's details first. The route's card also has **On the profile**, **Avoid this spot** and **The whole route**, which has what **More** has. After you add a point and stop for a moment, up to two other ways to it may appear on the map in blue, each labelled with how much longer or shorter it is and its share of trails; your route's label shows its own share, so you can compare. When you take your time between points they come together; otherwise the second can follow a few seconds later. Select one to take it, or carry on and the route stays as it is. After you take one, the way you had stays, grey, until your next point, so you can go back. **Other ways** in **Edit this stretch** shows up to two for one leg. Selecting your route with the planner closed opens it again. Undo and redo let you try changes.

**Hold the map still for half a second** (a ring fills under your finger), or **right-click** it, to see what's there: the card says what the map has at that spot (a path, water, a place), how far it is from your route, its height and your sport's fact of the way (the difficulty, the trail grade or the surface), and has **What's here**, **Your route** and a row with **Share**, **Weather here ›** and **Not right?**. Let go sooner and it's a tap; move your finger and it's the map's. **+ Add to my route** puts the spot where it adds the least to your route, between two points or after the end, never before the start, and says which point it follows; within 30 m of the line it's **Add a point here** on the line, and with no route **Start a route here**. **Start here** and **End here** add a new start or end without taking anything away. **Round trip from here** and **Avoid this spot** work from the spot too. With the route's view or the planner closed the card has no pill and no **Your route**. On a phone a card opens at the foot of the screen, with the planner closed too: **⌄** folds it to a line, and **✕** closes it, leaving the planner closed. A tap on a place's icon (a shelter, a campfire site, a toilet, a peak, an obstacle, a LIPAS place) shows its card, even while you draw. On a route you draw, **hold the line still** (or right-click it) for **Edit this stretch**; hold it a moment and then drag to reshape it. **What's here** says what the map knows, your sport's details of the way first and what it doesn't know said so, and has the spot's height and its coordinates with **Copy**; **All OpenStreetMap details** asks for the map's own data, once. **Not right?** opens OpenStreetMap's note page at the spot (no account needed), or its editor for that way or place. **Share** opens your phone's share sheet with a link to the spot, its name and its coordinates, or copies the link; the link opens Polku at the spot with a pin and its card, and never carries your route or where you are. **Weather here ›** opens the timeline held at the spot until you close it or pick another spot.

**With a keyboard,** Tab to the map: a crosshair shows in its middle, with the keys listed on the map. The arrows move the map, **Enter** does what a tap does at the crosshair (a point while you draw, the route's card on the route, a place's card on its icon), **Shift+F10** or the menu key what holding does, and **Esc** closes the card and gives the map back the focus.

The planner has three tabs:

- **Route:** **How you go** (the route style, one for each sport on a multisport route) and **Your speed**, each a step of its own with **Back**, then the list: sections where the sport changes, at a stop and at a night (then a section a day), each with its distance, time and climb, the weather at its end and its warnings; a row for each point you see and for each stretch between them, the points that only shape a stretch folded under it. **Reorder** lists every point with its grip, **Earlier** and **Later**: a mouse drags by the grip, and a finger holds a row a moment first. On a desktop ↑ and ↓ move between rows, Alt+↑ and Alt+↓ move a point, and Delete removes it.
- **Check:** the key facts, warnings, climbs, ground conditions and route colours.
- **Go:** **Leave**, when you'd be there and where the time goes, **Your speed**, the weather, GPX export, sharing and offline save.

Under the route's numbers a line says what stands out on this route: a water leg's wind, rain on the way, a headwind that adds a quarter of an hour, or unpaved on a road bike; otherwise the sport's usual fact (a trip's hardest day, the hardest climb on a bike, the high point on foot). Weather that isn't known is said beside it. A route of two sports has a row for each. A tap opens where it's told in full, and Check's top keeps them all. In **Climbs**, the hardest has a **Hardest** tag, by the uphill grade on an MTB or gravel bike and by height times gradient otherwise.

In **Check → On the route**, available colour kinds are grouped into **Terrain**, **Ground**, **Weather**, and **Route** for mixed sports. Groups sit side by side in a wide panel and stack on phones. Each kind has a bar, legend and distances. The title and bar form one compact button: tap either (**Use colours**) to select it in **Colour by** and colour the route on the map as well as the profile; the current choice says **Selected**. The legend below lines up category distances. Tap a group header to fold or open all its bars and legends together. Weather bars appear once the forecast arrives; kinds without data or that do not apply to the sport are left out.

With a mouse, **hover a bar segment or its legend row** to highlight every matching stretch on the map. This works even if another colouring is selected. Moving away clears the preview without changing your colours or moving the map. With a keyboard, Tab to a breakdown button: Left/Right steps through its categories, Home/End previews the first/last, and Escape or moving focus away clears the preview. Enter selects the colours as usual. Touch taps on the title or bar keep their colour-selection action and do not leave a hover preview.

Under **Your route** in all layers, while a route is drawn, distance marks, arrival times and elapsed times default on. Arrival is the clock time you reach a mark; elapsed time is the hours, minutes and seconds since departure, including stops and changeovers. Switch them off separately if needed; saved choices are kept.

**Leave**, on the start's card and in Go, opens your phone's own date and time picker (a click on a desktop opens the browser's), for any day ahead. Tap the summary's time estimate to open Go at it. Once set, it says the day and the time, and **Now** on its line leaves now again. Within its 48 hours the weather timeline shows the same hour, and moving the timeline moves the departure. The date stays with the route on this device and in share links, and **Share link** says so; set **Now** first to share without it. A route or a link whose date has passed opens leaving now, saying when it was planned for. **Export GPX** writes the planned times in each waypoint's description, none on the track.

The forecast reaches ten days ahead. Past it the profile goes grey in the weather's colours, with **No forecast yet** where it ends; Check's **Not checked** says from where, and the wind, rain, cold and waves aren't checked there. A start past it says **No forecast yet** for the trip; sunset and the dark still count.

The route glows on the map, and white chevrons drift along it the way it goes; they rest when you leave the map alone, and **Settings → Display → Moving arrows on the route** turns them off.

On a phone the planner has four sizes: its line, the peek with the route's numbers, half and full. Swipe it up or down from its handle, its top rows (over the buttons too), its tabs, or its list when the list is scrolled to the top. Let go slowly and it settles at the size nearest where you let go; a flick moves it one size. While you drag, after a moment, a mark at the screen's left edge shows each size, and a line names the one it will go to. At half and full the whole planner scrolls, the route's numbers too, under the row with the handle and ⌄: once the numbers have scrolled away that row shows them and the route's name, a swipe on that row moves the planner, and at the top a swipe down lowers it. On a computer the side panel scrolls as one too, and once the numbers have scrolled away a row at its top shows the route's name, its numbers and **⌄**. A tap on the handle folds it down or opens it again. In a narrow window on a computer, or with the planner at the bottom, a mouse drags it the same way by its handle, its top rows, its tabs or its folded line, and **⌃** beside **⌄** moves it one size up, **⌄** one size down.

To see the map, fold the planner to one line: **⌄** at its top right, or a swipe down from its smallest size. The line shows how many things there are to read before you go and the route's numbers, and says **Routing…** while a leg is still being routed; a tap anywhere on it, or a swipe up, opens the planner as it was. A second swipe down, or **✕** at the line's end, puts the planner away: the route stays on the map, and **Open** in the note or **Plan** brings the planner back. A single swipe always stops at the line. Folded, the toolbox keeps how the next leg is drawn, **Undo** and **Redo**, since a tap on the map still adds a point. A card, such as a point's, folds the same way to its name and its first two numbers (a point's: when you get there, and how far from the start); a card without numbers says something of its own, such as the whole route's distance and time, a place's kind, the sport chosen or how many places to sleep it found. A card opens no taller than the planner's peek; a swipe up, on the card itself too, takes it to half, and another to its whole height, full at most, where it scrolls first (a card shorter than the peek opens at its own height and stays so). From its line, a swipe down or ✕ closes it and the planner is as it was. In the route's view, **⋯** is at the left of ⌄'s row when the planner is open all the way. Where the planner would cover more than half the screen, as at 200 % zoom, it rests at its line.

On a desktop, **⌄** in the planner's header folds it down to its line at the foot of the map, with the toolbar beside it, and **✕** puts it away; with the planner at the bottom, to its line with the toolbar on it. A card on the map folds to its title row with its own ⌄, saying the same beside its name, keeping its foot where it was. The button in the planner's header next to ⌄ moves the planner between the side and bottom of the map.

## Read the map

**Layers** opens a quick drawer: the base maps as pictures, and five tiles for your sport (MTB's are trail colours, trail details, official routes, Waymarked MTB and rain radar). A tap on a map or a tile changes the map at once. A tile with **›** has more settings (the kind of trail colours, the opacity), one step in; **‹** comes back. A tile says **Zoom in to see** while its layer doesn't show at this zoom, and **No data now** while its source isn't answering. **Edit** changes the five tiles for that sport to any layer in the full panel; **Reset** puts back the sport's own. **More** opens every layer and the map keys, and says how many other layers are on. On a phone the drawer is a sheet: swipe it down or tap the map to close it (that tap adds no point to a route), swipe it up for every layer. On a desktop it stays open beside the button while you use the map.

**3D** in the map's buttons, under zoom (on a phone, under locate), raises the terrain and tilts the map; another tap lays it flat again. It's filled and underlined while 3D is on. It's the same setting as **3D terrain** in **Layers → More**, where you can also set how much the terrain is raised.

**Settings → Display → Appearance** sets how Polku looks. **Automatic**, the default, is light or dark as your device is, and changes when the device does, also while Polku is open. **Light** and **Dark** keep Polku that way, whatever the device says. **Match the map** makes Polku dark over the aerial photos and the night map, and light over the Trail map, Plain, Maastokartta and MML, and changes as you pick another map; the map itself never changes with it. The choice stays on this device. **High contrast** is a setting of its own: it thickens the trails and turns the glass that floats over the map solid, with darker grey words, heavier lines and a clear outline round each panel and button. Your device's settings for more contrast or less transparency turn the glass solid too.

Trail difficulty uses mountain biking grades **S0–S6**; uphill difficulty uses **↑S0–↑S5**, with arrows pointing uphill. Select a trail for its details. On a desktop, resting the pointer on a feature gives a quick preview, and a marked route lights up along its whole length while the pointer is on it. A route you tap on the map stays lit while its card is open.

Search finds places, official LIPAS routes and stops, trails and coordinates. In a narrow window, such as a phone zoomed to 150 % or 200 %, the search is its magnifier: a tap on it opens the field across the top bar, and Escape or a result folds it back. **Follow this route** puts an official route into the planner using its recorded line; **Add to route** joins it to your existing plan.

Route warnings open Check in three groups: **Read before you go** (red boxes: what can stop you, turn you back or hurt), **Not checked** (a source that didn't answer, with **Retry**) and **Worth a look**, each in order along the route, then **What Polku looked at**. A tap on a warning shows its card: where it is and how long, where it comes from, its note and its one action; on a phone **‹ ›** step along the route. **Avoid it** asks the planner to route around that place. When a route goes round a way marked private, its card says how much a short cut would save, and **Short cut** lets that leg through it (the leg's route style becomes **Ways marked private: Short cuts ok**). Use it only where you may go: private usually means a factory or military site, a house yard or a road closed by a sign, though in Finland private roads and apartment-block yards are open on foot and by bike. Weather along the route uses the estimated time you reach each section, so check your departure in Go and your speeds in Route.

At the planner's peek, a row above the numbers says how many warnings to read before you go and how many weren't checked; a tap shows the first on the route. **✕** on the row marks them all read (with **Undo**), **Got it** on a warning's card marks one, and **All read** marks a group in Check. With large text on a small phone, or on a phone on its side, the count sits beside the numbers instead, and **All read** in Check marks them all. Read warnings stay in Check, saying so, and on the map; a new or changed one brings the row back. A warning's card ends with a way to check or correct where it comes from, and **Retry** says it's asking until the source answers. Markers on the route show the warnings: a red triangle to read before you go, a dashed "?" where a source didn't answer, and, while Check is open, an eye for what's worth a look. A tap on a marker shows its card. With the planner closed, a tap on a triangle opens its card with **Open the planner at it**.

## Keyboard shortcuts

Shortcuts work when you are not typing in an input field.

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| <kbd>T</kbd> | Toggle the weather timeline |
| <kbd>L</kbd> | Open or close the quick layer drawer (the maps, your sport's tiles, More for every layer); the arrow keys choose a map |
| <kbd>R</kbd> | Open or close the route planner |
| <kbd>E</kbd> | **Done** or **Edit**: end editing for the route's view, or go back to drawing |
| <kbd>F</kbd> | Show the whole route |
| <kbd>P</kbd>, <kbd>S</kbd>, <kbd>W</kbd> | While editing: draw the next leg with Follow paths, a straight line or over water |
| <kbd>/</kbd> | Focus search |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>/<kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>Z</kbd> | Undo |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>/<kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Z</kbd> | Redo |
| <kbd>Esc</kbd> | Close open panels and menus; in Layers, one step back first |

## Map gestures

Pinching, or Ctrl+scrolling, over a panel zooms the map. In 3D, a two-finger trackpad scroll tilts and rotates; Shift+scroll tilts anywhere. You can also use the orbit pad, right-drag or a two-finger touch gesture.

## Before going offline

Use **Save offline** in Go or the planner's **More** (⋯). Then use **Preview offline** to check the saved coverage. On an iPhone, add Polku to the home screen to help keep the saved data available.

The saved map, terrain, route and profile work without a connection; routing, search, weather and traffic do not. Read the [limitations](../README.md#limitations), especially before planning on water.

## What’s new

Open **Settings → About Polku → What’s new** to read dated release notes over the map. The route stays as it was. Scroll the notes, use their links to read more, and close with **Close** or **Escape** to return to Settings.

The notes load only when you open the window. Their latest date is shown; a saved copy may appear if you are offline or the connection is slow. If none can load, reconnect and select **Retry**, or use **Full changelog on GitHub**.
