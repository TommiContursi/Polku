# Using Polku

[Open Polku](https://polku.si) · [Overview](../README.md)

## Plan and edit a route

Open **Plan**, choose a sport and add waypoints on the map. Drag a waypoint or the route line to change the plan. On a phone, hold the line before dragging it.

With no route yet, the planner offers four ways to begin under **Start a route** and your sport: **Draw it**, **Round trip**, **Marked routes** and **A file**. The sport there is the same as the button beside the search. A tap or click on the map starts drawing at once. Each way has **‹ Ways to begin** at its top left, which brings the four back; on a phone the back gesture does the same. The sport is in a row of its own under the top row, in **Start a route** and in each way, and **When** under it: **Leave now**, or a tap for its menu, where **Leave at…** opens your phone's own date and time picker; on a desktop it shows a date and time field, and **Set** or Enter sets the time. The time you pick is the one Go's **Leave** sets, so the route you draw next leaves then, and it stays when you reload Polku. A time that has passed leaves now, and the row says when it was planned for; **✕** at the top right closes the planner.

- **Draw it** says how Polku joins your waypoints in the drawing mode you've chosen, and lets you pick the route style before the first leg; **Fine-tune** opens Route style's controls there. A style picked before there's a route routes nothing.
- **Round trip:** from the ways to begin, it first asks for its start: tap the map, or use **Where I am**, which sends your position to the router as the start, rounded to about a metre. With a start, the rest of the form appears: **How long**, by **Distance** or **Time**, with the sport's lengths (three on a phone, four on a desktop) or **Other…** for your own (typed, or **−** and **+**); then the route style and the direction. Then **Make 3 loops**, which says what it costs in routing calls. After your first waypoint, **Round trip from here** offers three lengths and **Other…**. While the loops are made the map waits. Tap a loop's card or its line on the map to pick it, and **Use loop A** to take it; **Undo** takes it back.
- **Marked routes** lists LIPAS's routes for your sport in the map's view. Tap a card or a route's line to pick it, and **Follow this route** to make it your route on its own line. It says when the map doesn't show them (**Show them**), when LIPAS isn't answering (**Retry**) and when there are none in view. **Find by name** searches all of Finland.

The planner's header shows your route: its distance, time and climb. The time is the whole of it, its stops, changeovers and nights too, and where those make a difference the time on the move is under it ("38 min" over "8 min moving"), else when you leave; a tap on it sets the departure, and **Go** breaks it down. How the next leg is drawn is in the toolbox, with undo, redo and **More** (⋯). The toolbox grows with the route: before the first waypoint it has only undo and redo, and only when there's something to undo; from the first waypoint, the drawing mode and **More**; once there's a leg, the route's actions too. While a round trip is open it has only undo and redo, until you take a loop or close it.

- **On a phone** the toolbox floats above the planner: **Done** first, as on a desktop, then the drawing button, undo, redo, the route's actions that fit in the window's width and **More**. Where it would run under the map's buttons, they rise above it. With the planner raised all the way it's the planner's first row. The drawing button shows the mode, and under it the next leg's sport and route style ("Hike · Balanced"). Tap it to draw the next legs with **Follow paths**, **Freehand** or **Over water**, to pick their sport and see your speed in it, or to pick a route style for the whole route. On a phone narrower than about 357 px it shows only the mode, then shortens it; at 200 % it's the mode's icon alone, and the toolbox takes a second row.
- **On a desktop** it's a rounded toolbar at the map's foot, floating 12 px from the planner's edge (above it, with the planner at the bottom), with the map's scale and credits just above it: **Done** at the planner's end, then the three modes, the next leg's sport, the route style, undo and redo, the route's actions that fit, **Clear route** and **More**. Point at a button, or reach it with the keyboard, for its name, its key and what it does. In a narrow window the three modes show only their icons first, then more of the route's actions move into More (**Clear route** last), the sport shows only its icon, and in the narrowest the toolbar takes a second row. On a big screen it has every name and every action. Moving the planner between the side and the bottom frames your route again.

**Done** ends editing and keeps your route open to read, check and take along: the route's view. Its header is the same as while you draw: the route's numbers, with **✕** at the end of their row on a desktop; on a phone, **⌄** folds the planner to its line, whose **✕** puts it away. A route's own name, from a file or a picked loop, heads the list in **Route**: "MTB · Pyynikki loop". The toolbar reads only **Edit**, with its pencil, and **More**, on a phone and a desktop alike, **Edit** where **Done** was: the view is for reading the route, and what changes it is one step away, in **More** or on a card. With the planner raised all the way on a phone, **Edit** and **More** are at the left of its top row, beside the handle. A tap on the map then shows what's there instead of adding a waypoint, a tap on your route opens its card, and the waypoints stay where they are when you drag them. That is all **Done** and **Edit** change: the cards still change the route, so **Move**, **Remove**, **Rename**, **Sport from here**, **Edit this stretch**, **Avoid this spot**, **Add a waypoint here** and **Add to my route** work at once, and the planner stays in the route's view. **Route** has the same sections, without **Reorder**. **Edit**, where **Done** was, goes back to drawing, with undo as it was. A round trip, a file and a marked route to follow as a new route go back to editing on their own. With only your first waypoint, **Done** is dimmed, as there's no route to read yet; **✕** closes the planner. With the planner folded to its line, **Edit** and **More** stay above it. A shared route opens in the route's view.

**✕** closes the planner. Your route stays on the map, and a note says so, with **Open**. **Plan** opens the planner again as you left it: in the route's view or editing, at the size you left it. After a reload it opens again if it was open.

A mode changes only the legs you draw next. To change a drawn leg, select it on the map. A route style is for the whole route and routes it again; a leg with a style of its own keeps it. The route's actions are a share link, GPX export and saving offline; reversing it and a leg back to the start; and **Clear route**, which a note lets you undo. While you edit, those that fit are on the toolbox or toolbar, and **More** has the rest, with a round trip from the start and importing a GPX or KML file, which are only ever there. On a desktop **Clear route** is a button just before **More**, kept there longest, as a pointer doesn't slip onto it and its note has Undo. **More** has one order on a phone and a desktop: the whole route's actions, then taking it along, then **Round trip from the start**, **Import GPX or KML…** and **Clear route** together, the three that start again, **Clear route** last. **Show the whole route** is under **Locate** with the map's buttons whenever a route is on the map, on a desktop too.

### Sports along a route

A route can change sport from one leg to the next: ride to the shore, paddle across, then run. **Draw it** says so before the first waypoint.

- **The next legs:** pick their sport in the drawing menu (on a desktop, the sport button on the toolbar). What you've drawn stays as it is, the map switches to that sport, and Paddle and Swim draw over water. Where the sport changes, the time includes a changeover. Under the sport is your speed in it; **Change** opens your speeds right there, and a change re-times the route.
- **The button beside the search** sets the map's sport. With a route in another sport, it asks what the new sport is for: **The whole route** plans the route's legs again in it, says how many routing calls that takes, and **Undo** brings everything back; **Only the legs you draw next** keeps the route as it is; **Keep** changes nothing. A route on land can't become a water route that way: change a single leg from its menu on the map instead.
- **A waypoint on open water:** while you draw on land, Polku asks how you get there: **Paddle**, **Swim** or **Stay on land**, and in winter over the ice too. Paddle and Swim take the leg to the waypoint over the water and keep the waypoint where you tapped it; the legs after it carry on in that sport. A leg over the ice is a straight line, and Check says that Polku can't see whether the ice holds: check the ice yourself before you go. A waypoint on a bridge or a ferry line, or right by the shore, doesn't ask.
- **The map's sport** doesn't change your route: the route keeps its sports and its own route style for each of them.
- **Your speeds** for every sport are also in **Settings → Speeds and tiles by sport**, one line each; tap a sport to change them. Polku times your route with them.
- **Your craft** for paddling (**Sea kayak**, **Canoe** or **SUP**) is in the sport menus when the sport is Paddle, and in your speeds. Polku keeps a canoe or a SUP to calmer water than a sea kayak.

While you plan, **Weather** and <kbd>T</kbd> open the weather timeline over the map, and the planner stays where it was. On a phone the timeline sits just above the planner, with the toolbox and the map's buttons above it; with the planner pulled up to half or more, it sits on the planner's top until you close it. Go keeps the departure and the weather along the route.

Tap or click a waypoint for its card, or hold it for half a second, or right-click it: each opens the same card, and so does a tap on its row in Route's list. It has its name, when you'd get there, how far it is from the start and its height, and right under them **Move**, **Rename** and **Remove** (in red), in sight as the card opens; the rest of what you can do there is further down the card. **At this waypoint** switches between **Pass by**, **Stop** (its minutes with − and +) and **Night** (**Stay**, the nights with − and +; **Go on at**, which opens your phone's clock, its line the day you go on; and **Places to sleep nearby**). The start's card has **Leave** (below). **Sport from here** changes the legs from the waypoint to the next change of sport, or the end; **Paddle** has your craft under it, for all your paddle legs, and a change of sport has its changeover minutes and **Take the bike along**. **Next stretch, to …** opens that stretch's card. Then the route's profile, coloured up to the waypoint and grey after it, with **On the profile** under it (the legs on both sides of the waypoint in the planner's profile), and **More for this waypoint**: **Start here** and **End here**, which say what they remove, and on the start and the end **Reverse** and **Back to start**. **Move** asks you to tap where the waypoint goes (**Cancel** or **Escape** leaves it), **Rename** types its name where it's shown, and **Remove** has Undo in its note. On a phone the card is in the planner and only undo and redo stay beside it; on a desktop it opens at the waypoint, and the waypoint's row in the list lights up. **✕**, **Escape**, the back gesture or a tap on the map closes it, the planner as it was, and that tap adds no waypoint. In the route's view the card has all of this too, and its changes keep the route's view.

Tap or click the route for its card: the way there, three numbers for your sport (when you'd be there, how steep, and the difficulty, the trail grade or the surface; on the water the wind and temperature), its actions under them, and the stretch's most serious warning. While you draw, a tap or click just beside the route opens its card too, so a near miss never adds a point at the end: that reaches about 24 px from the line with a finger and 16 px with a mouse, so to add a waypoint near the route, or to carry on from its end, tap a little farther away. **Add a waypoint here**, in green, adds one at the spot: on a phone it's under the numbers and a pin marks the spot, on a desktop it's the card's first button, by the pointer. **Avoid this spot** is under the numbers, and **Other ways** where BRouter routed the leg. **This stretch** runs to the next waypoint you see, over the waypoints that only shape the line, with its numbers and what to look out for; **Edit this stretch** sets its sport, how it's drawn and its style in one step of **Undo**, and has **Other ways**, **Stop here**, **Avoid this spot** and **On the profile**. **What's here** lists what the map knows of the way, your sport's details first. **More for this leg** has **On the profile** and **The whole route**, which has what **More** has. After you add a waypoint and stop for a moment, up to two other ways to it may appear on the map in blue, each labelled with how much longer or shorter it is and its share of trails; your route's label shows its own share, so you can compare. When you take your time between waypoints they come together; otherwise the second can follow a few seconds later. Select one to take it, or carry on and the route stays as it is. After you take one, the way you had stays, grey, until your next waypoint, so you can go back. **Other ways** in **Edit this stretch** shows up to two for one leg. Selecting your route with the planner closed opens it again. Undo and redo let you try changes. While the route's card is open, the leg you tapped (between the two numbered waypoints around it) stands out on the map and the rest of the route fades; a stretch's card lights its whole stretch. On a desktop, pointing at the route lights the leg under the pointer the same way.

**Tap or click a way, or hold the map still for half a second** (a ring fills under your finger) or **right-click** it, to see what's there. A tap on a street, a trail or a cycleway picks the whole of it, not a piece: the pieces that continue each other join (unnamed paths stop at every junction; a cycleway goes on past side streets' crossings, straight on), and it is drawn on the map like a route. On a phone, while you're not drawing, the tap first shows a line above the spot (its name, how you get along it, its height and grade there, the weather); a tap on the line opens the card, and a tap elsewhere or **Escape** takes it away. On a desktop, resting the pointer on a way says the same and draws the way; a click opens the card. Its card has the same facts whatever your sport: its length, up and down, and under **Profile** the way's height profile, coloured like the map: point at it, or run a finger along it, and it says how far along, how high and how steep, with the spot shown on the map; the pointer on the drawn way shows its place on the profile. **Profile** folds with its arrow and stays folded until you open it again; folded, it says what the map's colours show, and the weather along the way isn't asked for. **Colour by**, a list under the profile, has every fact that changes along the way (gradient, uphill difficulty, MTB and hiking difficulty, surface, way type, smoothness, winter care, width, visibility, wind, temperature, rain, cycling, one way, lit and speed limit), each only where the way has it, with the words of each colour and its length. Then the facts in groups (**The way**, **Surface**, **Difficulty**, **Weather here now**): **Cycling** (shared with walkers, separated from walkers, a bike path, a bicycle lane, a footway…, which the line under its name says too, after **Cycling:**), with a sign before it and before one way, and a grade as a badge in its difficulty's colour, the places at the spot, and one quiet line, **Not mapped: …**, with **Not right?**. The card opens at once with what the map's own tile knows and fills in as OpenStreetMap, the terrain and the forecast answer: while they come, it says what it's waiting for, grey shapes hold the places of its numbers and profile (**Profile** folds then too), and the way is drawn and measured as far as it has come, its length **long so far** until all of it is in. Until OpenStreetMap answers, the piece of the way under your finger is drawn from the map's own data, and the card and the line above the spot say the whole way is loading; after 10 seconds they say OpenStreetMap is slow to answer, and if it doesn't answer, that piece stays, the line says so, and **Try again** on the card asks again. A tap on a lake or a park, with no way near, picks it: its outline is drawn, and its card has its name and kind, its height, how far round its edge (where all of its outline came: a big lake's is drawn for some kilometres around the spot), and what OpenStreetMap says of it. Under its numbers, however you opened it, is a row of four round buttons: **Add to my route**, in green, puts the spot where it adds the least to your route, between two waypoints or after the end, never before the start, and says which waypoint it follows (within 30 m of the line it's **Add a waypoint here**, and with no route **Start a route here**), and a place gives the waypoint its name; then **Avoid this spot**, **Share** and **Weather here**. **More for this place** (or spot, or way) has **Start here** and **End here**, which add a new start or end without taking anything away, **Round trip from here**, **Paddle from here** over water, **Copy** (the spot's coordinates) and **All OpenStreetMap details**, which asks for the map's own data, once. **⋯** beside **✕** is **Card settings**: what the profile is coloured by, and whether the route's colours show on the map. On a phone a card opens at the foot of the screen with the planner's sizes, with the planner closed too: **⌄** folds it to a line, and **✕** closes it, leaving the planner closed. Let go of a hold sooner and it's a tap; move your finger and it's the map's. Road works, an incident, road conditions, a busy road, a military area and a sea mark open the same card, with the thing ringed or drawn on the map. The water's card has its depth from Traficom's chart and **Paddle from here**, on a lake, on the sea and when OpenStreetMap doesn't answer. A tap on a place's icon (a shelter, a campfire site, a toilet, a peak, an obstacle, a LIPAS place) shows its card, even while you draw. On a route you draw, **hold the line still** (or right-click it) for **Edit this stretch**; hold it a moment and then drag to reshape it. **Not right?**, at the card's foot, opens OpenStreetMap's note page at the spot (no account needed), or its editor for that way or place. **Share** opens your phone's share sheet with a link to the spot, its name and its coordinates, or copies the link; the link opens Polku at the spot with a pin and its card, and never carries your route or where you are. **Weather here ›** opens the timeline held at the spot until you close it or pick another spot.

**With a keyboard,** Tab to the map: a crosshair shows in its middle, with the keys listed on the map. The arrows move the map, **Enter** does what a tap does at the crosshair (a waypoint while you draw, the route's card on the route, a place's card on its icon), **Shift+F10** or the menu key what holding does (the same card), and **Esc** closes the card and gives the map back the focus. Tab from the map goes on to the waypoints, and **Enter** on one opens its card.

The planner has three tabs:

- **Route:** the list first, the route drawn as one line through every waypoint, each sport's part in its colour: parts where the sport changes and at a night (then a part a day), each with a heading (the sport in a ring of its colour, its distance, time and climb under it, its stops added to the time: "8 min + 30 min stop", and a **⚙** at its end), a button: a tap opens the part's **Sport**, which changes only its legs, its sport's **Your speed** and its **Route style**; a row for each waypoint you see, with how far it is from the start and how long it takes to get there, stops and changeovers included, in a column left of the route's line ("1.11 km" over "5 min"; under its name when the text is enlarged), and, with **Weather** shown, the weather then at each waypoint as icons (the time on the clock is in its card, and on a desktop when you hover it); each part after the first starts with the waypoint you set off from again; a changeover, a stop and a night are rows of their own, with their times (a night's sunset and sunrise too) and the gear you leave there; for each stretch between waypoints its distance, time and climb, Check's warnings on it, each named in Check's words, its places counted, three lines at most (a stretch with waypoints folded in it names one or two, and counts more: warnings, not checked, good to know) and, with the details below shown, its average speed, how it's drawn and how much of it is after sunset, in the dark or before sunrise; the waypoints that only shape a stretch folded under it as a bead each on its line, their number beside **⌄**; unfolded, the stretch gives way to its legs, a row for each leg between two waypoints with its own numbers, how it's drawn and its warnings, which opens that leg's card (**Edit this stretch** there changes that leg alone). Over the list, which has no heading of its own (the **Route** tab says it), **Show all waypoints** in green, with how many are hidden beside it, unfolds every stretch's waypoints (**Fold all waypoints** folds them), a stretch's **⌄** in green before its count does the same for that stretch, and **List settings** (the sliders) choose the times, **From start** or **Clock**, and what the rows show: **Warnings** and **Good to know** (Check's lighter ones and the gear left at a changeover; the only two on at first), **Drawing mode** (Follow paths, Freehand), **Darkness**, **Weather** and **Speed** (each stretch's average, as **Your speed** gives it), each a switch, remembered, and the same in **Settings › Route’s list**; hidden, they're still in Check and the cards. **✕** closes the menu, and so does a tap outside it, which does nothing else. On a desktop, hovering a stretch shows where it is along the route, when, its average speed and what the profile says over all of it (the grade, the hardest uphill and downhill, the way, the wind and the weather, with the sky's icon, as in the profile's tip); a waypoint, its times and the profile's facts there; a stop, a night or a changeover, where, when and the weather as you go on. There, a waypoint's row also shows **Rename**, **Remove** and a grip where its › is (with the keyboard's focus too): Rename makes its name a field in the row (Enter or clicking away keeps it, Escape doesn't), Remove takes it out with **Undo** in the note, and the grip drags it up or down the list. Under the list are **Reorder**, **Reverse**, **Back to start** (not on a route that already comes back) and **Clear route** in a row, Clear route a red button at its end, with **Undo** in its note; **Round trip** and **Import** are in **More** (⋯) and on the route's cards, and **Back to start** is there too. **Reorder** lists every waypoint with its grip, **Earlier** and **Later**, and on a route of more than one sport the start's sport and each change with its minutes ("MTB → Paddle · 15 min"), so you see where a move takes it: a mouse drags by the grip, and a finger holds a row a moment first; the same button, **Done**, ends it. On a desktop ↑ and ↓ move between rows, Alt+↑ and Alt+↓ move a waypoint, and Delete removes it. Under the list (beside it in a wide bottom panel), **Route style** (one for each sport on a multisport route, with the sport's icon in its colour and its name) and **Your speed**, each a step of its own with **Back**.
- **Check:** the key facts, warnings, climbs, ground conditions and route colours.
- **Go:** **Leave**, when you'd be there and where the time goes, **Your speed**, the weather, GPX export, sharing and offline save.

Under the route's numbers a line says what stands out on this route: a water leg's wind, rain on the way, a headwind that adds a quarter of an hour, or unpaved on a road bike; otherwise the sport's usual fact (a trip's hardest day, the hardest climb on a bike, the high point on foot). Weather that isn't known is said beside it. A route of two sports has a row for each. A tap opens where it's told in full, and Check's top keeps them all. In **Climbs**, the hardest has a **Hardest** tag, by the uphill grade on an MTB or gravel bike and by height times gradient otherwise.

In **Check → On the route**, available colour kinds are grouped into **Terrain**, **Ground**, **Weather**, and **Route** for mixed sports. Groups sit side by side in a wide panel and stack on phones. Each kind has a bar, legend and distances. The title and bar form one compact button: tap either (**Use colours**) to select it in **Colour by** and colour the route on the map as well as the profile; the current choice says **Selected**. The legend below lines up category distances. Tap a group header to fold or open all its bars and legends together. Weather bars appear once the forecast arrives; kinds without data or that do not apply to the sport are left out.

With a mouse, **hover a bar segment or its legend row** to highlight every matching stretch on the map. This works even if another colouring is selected. Moving away clears the preview without changing your colours or moving the map. With a keyboard, Tab to a breakdown button: Left/Right steps through its categories, Home/End previews the first/last, and Escape or moving focus away clears the preview. Enter selects the colours as usual. Touch taps on the title or bar keep their colour-selection action and do not leave a hover preview.

Under **Your route** in all layers, while a route is drawn, distance marks, arrival times and elapsed times default on. Arrival is the clock time you reach a mark; elapsed time is the hours, minutes and seconds since departure, including stops and changeovers. Switch them off separately if needed; saved choices are kept.

**Leave**, on the start's card and in Go, opens your phone's own date and time picker (a click on a desktop opens the browser's), for any day ahead. Tap the summary's time estimate to open Go at it. Once set, it says the day and the time, and **Now** on its line leaves now again. Within its 48 hours the weather timeline shows the same hour, and moving the timeline moves the departure. The date stays with the route on this device and in share links, and **Share link** says so; set **Now** first to share without it. A route or a link whose date has passed opens leaving now, saying when it was planned for. **Export GPX** writes the planned times in each waypoint's description, none on the track.

The forecast reaches ten days ahead. Past it the profile goes grey in the weather's colours, with **No forecast yet** where it ends; Check's **Not checked** says from where, and the wind, rain, cold and waves aren't checked there. A start past it says **No forecast yet** for the trip; sunset and the dark still count.

The route glows on the map, and white chevrons drift along it the way it goes; they rest when you leave the map alone, and **Settings → Display → Moving arrows on the route** turns them off.

On a phone the planner has four sizes: its line, the peek, half and full, and every card has the same four, so a swipe always lands in the same place. The peek has the route's numbers and, under them, the route's profile, while you draw and in the route's view; tap the profile to open the planner at half, where you can run a finger along it. Swipe the planner up or down from its handle, its top rows (over the buttons too), the profile at the peek, its tabs, or its list when the list is scrolled to the top. Let go slowly and it settles at the size nearest where you let go; a flick moves it one size. At half and full the whole planner scrolls, the route's numbers too, under the row with the handle and ⌄: once the numbers have scrolled away that row shows them and the route's name, a swipe on that row moves the planner, and at the top a swipe down lowers it. On a computer the side panel scrolls as one too, and once the numbers have scrolled away a row at its top shows the route's name, its numbers and **⌄**. A tap on the handle folds it down or opens it again. In a narrow window on a computer, or with the planner at the bottom, a mouse drags it the same way by its handle, its top rows, its tabs or its folded line, and **⌃** beside **⌄** moves it one size up, **⌄** one size down.

To see the map, fold the planner to one line: **⌄** at its top left, or a swipe down from its smallest size. **✕** at its top right puts it away. A card has its **⋯**, **⌄** and **✕** on one row beside its name. The line shows how many warnings there are and the route's numbers, and says **Routing…** while a leg is still being routed; a tap anywhere on it, or a swipe up, opens the planner as it was. A second swipe down, or **✕** at the line's end, puts the planner away: the route stays on the map, and **Open** in the note or **Plan** brings the planner back. A single swipe always stops at the line. Folded, the toolbox keeps how the next leg is drawn, as its icon, with **Undo**, **Redo** and **⋯**, since a tap on the map still adds a waypoint; the icon opens the drawing modes, the sport and the route style, and ⋯ the whole route's actions. A card, such as a waypoint's, folds the same way to its name and its first two numbers (a waypoint's: when you get there, and how far from the start); a card without numbers says something of its own, such as the whole route's distance and time, a place's kind, the sport chosen or how many places to sleep it found. A card opens at the peek with its actions in sight, the planner folded too (closing it folds the planner back), and always with its name and numbers; a swipe up, on the card itself too, takes it to half, and another to full, where it scrolls first. A short card has the same sizes and sits at the top, with room under it. A card opened from a card, such as **Next stretch**, and **Back** keep the size you raised it to. Back at its peek or its line, a card is at its top. From its line, a swipe down or ✕ closes it and the planner is as it was. Where the planner would cover more than half the screen, as at 200 % zoom, it rests at its line.

On a desktop, **⌄** in the planner's header folds it down to its line at the foot of the map, with the toolbar beside it, and **✕** puts it away; with the planner at the bottom, to its line with the toolbar on it. A card on the map folds to its title row with its own ⌄, saying the same beside its name, keeping its foot where it was. The button in the planner's header next to ⌄ moves the planner between the side and bottom of the map.

## Read the map

With editing off, a pointing-hand cursor shows roads, paths, routes and other map features you can click. Empty map space keeps the grab cursor. Rest the pointer briefly for a hint; fuller map details can follow after it appears.

The terrain maps keep more detail when you zoom out, and aerial photos use sharper images on high-density screens. Close-up detail still depends on the original photography. If a closer **Aerial** image is unavailable, the map enlarges the available coarser image. **Depth contours** appear from zoom level 10 and **Depth soundings** from level 13; the depths are displayed for planning, not navigation, and never used for routing.

**Layers** opens a quick drawer: the base maps as pictures, and five tiles for your sport (MTB's are trail colours, trail details, official routes, Waymarked MTB and rain radar). A tap on a map or a tile changes the map at once. A tile with **›** has more settings (the kind of trail colours, the opacity), one step in; **‹** comes back. A tile says **Zoom in to see** while its layer doesn't show at this zoom, and **No data now** while its source isn't answering. **Edit** changes the five tiles for that sport to any layer in the full panel; **Reset** puts back the sport's own. **Settings → Speeds and tiles by sport** lists each sport's tiles and opens the same Edit for that sport, whatever sport the map has. **More** opens every layer and the map keys, and says how many other layers are on. On a phone the drawer is a sheet: swipe it down or tap the map to close it (that tap adds no waypoint to a route), swipe it up for every layer. On a desktop it stays open beside the button while you use the map.

**3D** in the map's buttons, under zoom (on a phone, under locate), raises the terrain and tilts the map; another tap lays it flat again. It's filled and underlined while 3D is on. It's the same setting as **3D terrain** in **Layers → More**, where you can also set how much the terrain is raised; **Settings → Map and planner → 3D height** is the same setting.

**Settings → Display → Appearance** sets how Polku looks. **Automatic**, the default, is light or dark as your device is, and changes when the device does, also while Polku is open. **Light** and **Dark** keep Polku that way, whatever the device says. **Match the map** makes Polku dark over the aerial photos and the night map, and light over the Trail map, Plain, Maastokartta and MML, and changes as you pick another map; the map itself never changes with it. The choice stays on this device. **High contrast** is a setting of its own: it thickens the trails and turns the glass that floats over the map solid, with darker grey words, heavier lines and a clear outline round each panel and button. Your device's settings for more contrast or less transparency turn the glass solid too. **Fewer effects** turns only the glass solid, for a browser that scrolls a panel or moves the map slowly. When the map or a list keeps moving slowly, Polku offers it once on each device.

**Settings** keeps Polku's preferences in folding groups, which stay open or folded as you leave them: speeds and tiles by sport, Display, language and units, route arrows and marks, the map and the planner (with a row that opens **Layers**), the Heads-up kinds you've hidden with **Show again**, offline maps, what's saved on this device (**Clear** forgets your recent searches), Strava and Garmin, data sources and About Polku. Settings that are planned but not built yet are there already, greyed and marked **Coming**, so you can see what's on its way; they don't respond yet. On a phone Settings is a sheet from the bottom, like the layer drawer: swipe it down or tap the map to close it. Some settings are in two places, so you find them where you look: your speeds are in the planner too, the route's arrows and marks in **Layers** with your route, and 3D height in **Layers** under 3D terrain. Either place changes the same setting.

**Lit ways**, in **Layers → More → After dark** or as a tile you add, draws the ways OpenStreetMap says are lit after dark as a bright yellow line, and those it says aren't lit as a thin dashed grey line. A way with no line is one OpenStreetMap says nothing about, which isn't the same as dark. The lines load from zoom 14 and stay down to 13. Under its switch, **Lines · In the dark** picks the look. **In the dark** dims the map under a navy veil and lets the lit ways glow, with unlit ways still dashed; the veil covers only where the lighting has loaded. **Opacity** sets how strong either look is. While lighting loads, a message on the map shows how many areas have arrived, even with Layers closed. After ten seconds it says the wait is taking longer. If any areas fail, **Try again** asks for them again; the veil stays off those areas until their data arrives.

Trail difficulty uses mountain biking grades **S0–S6**; uphill difficulty uses **↑S0–↑S5**, with arrows pointing uphill. Select a trail for its details. On a desktop, resting the pointer on a feature gives a quick preview, and a marked route lights up along its whole length while the pointer is on it. A route you tap on the map stays lit while its card is open.

Search finds places, official LIPAS routes and stops, trails and coordinates. A place, coordinates or a trail you pick gets a pin and its card once the map is there (a trail's on its line); its card has the same actions (**Start a route here**, or **Add to my route**, **Start here** and **End here**), as the map's cards do, and a waypoint added from a place takes its name. In a narrow window, such as a phone zoomed to 150 % or 200 %, the search is its magnifier: a tap on it opens the field across the top bar, and Escape or a result folds it back. **Follow this route** puts an official route into the planner using its recorded line; **Add to route** joins it to your existing plan.

Route warnings open Check in three groups: **Warnings** (red boxes: what can stop you, turn you back or hurt), **Not checked** (a source that didn't answer, with **Retry**, or one not asked on a route that long) and **Good to know**, each in order along the route, then **What Polku looked at**. A tap on a warning shows its card: where it is and how long, where it comes from, its note and its one action; on a phone **‹ ›** step along the route. **Avoid it** asks the planner to route around that place. When a route goes round a way marked private, its card says how much a short cut would save, and **Short cut** lets that leg through it (the leg's route style becomes **Ways marked private: Short cuts ok**). Use it only where you may go: private usually means a factory or military site, a house yard or a road closed by a sign, though in Finland private roads and apartment-block yards are open on foot and by bike. Weather along the route uses the estimated time you reach each section, so check your departure in Go and your speeds in Route.

When Check finds a warning, the line under the route's numbers says how many ("2 warnings", or "1 not checked" when only a source didn't answer) in place of the route's key fact; a tap opens Check at the first, with its card. **Got it** on a warning's card marks one read, and **All read** marks a group in Check; with all of them read, the line shows the key fact again. With large text on a small phone, on a phone on its side, or in a very short window, where that line isn't shown, the count sits beside the numbers instead. Read warnings stay in Check, saying so, and on the map; a new or changed one brings the line back. A warning's card ends with a way to check or correct where it comes from, and **Retry** says it's asking until the source answers. Markers on the route show the warnings: a red triangle for a warning, a dashed "?" where a source didn't answer, and, while Check is open, an eye for what's worth a look. A tap on a marker shows its card. With the planner closed, a tap on a triangle opens its card with **Open the planner at it**.

## Map gestures

Pinching, or Ctrl+scrolling, over a panel zooms the map. The mouse wheel and a two-finger trackpad scroll up or down zoom, in 2D and 3D alike; a sideways trackpad scroll rotates, and Shift+scroll tilts. You can also use the orbit pad, right-drag or a two-finger touch gesture.

## Before going offline

Use **Save offline** in Go or the planner's **More** (⋯). Then use **Preview offline** to check the saved coverage. On an iPhone, add Polku to the home screen to help keep the saved data available.

The saved map, terrain, route and profile work without a connection; routing, search, weather and traffic do not. Read the [limitations](../README.md#limitations), especially before planning on water.

## What’s new

Open **Settings → About Polku → What’s new** to read dated release notes over the map. The route stays as it was. Scroll the notes, use their links to read more, and close with **Close** or **Escape** to return to Settings.

The notes load only when you open the window. Their latest date is shown; a saved copy may appear if you are offline or the connection is slow. If none can load, reconnect and select **Retry**, or use **Full changelog on GitHub**.
