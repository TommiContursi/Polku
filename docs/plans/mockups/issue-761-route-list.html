What Rows changes

  • A point is a row: its mark, its name, and one line: when you leave or get there, the weather then, a stop or a change of sport. Its distance from the start isn't on it (the stretch says how far). A tap opens its card (on a phone) or its popup (#760).
  • A plain stretch is a line between two points with its numbers in small type and ⌄ where it folds shaping points. A stretch that differs from a plain one (a straight line, over water, a LIPAS route or an imported track, a carry) has its icon and its word first, in the accent colour. A tap opens the stretch's card.
  • The sport's row heads its section: the sport, then its route style and its speed as values ("Balanced · Normal, 16 km/h"). A tap opens them. With two sports each section has its own row, and the point where the sport changes keeps its tag. How you go and Your speed leave the list's end.
  • Reorder sits under the list with Reverse, and Clear route on a row of its own under them (chosen on 2026-11-04; the switch shows it beside the sport's row, as today). On a desktop the grip reorders without it; the keyboard still needs it.
  • A desktop edits in the row: hover or focus a point's row and Rename and Remove appear where › was; the grip at its left drags it. On a phone the grip and the tools don't exist: the row opens the card, Reorder works as today.
  • Under the list: Reverse, and Clear route at the other end (chosen on 2026-11-04; the switch shows Reverse alone). Round trip, Back to start and Import are ways to begin or route actions: ⋯ and the start's card keep them.
  • The warning is in the key fact's slot under the numbers (#741), not a box in the list.

What Line changes (the second round)

  • One line runs from the start to the end through every waypoint's mark, as Komoot's does: each stretch is the line between two marks, green where it isn't along paths. Where the sport changes, the next sport's icon sits on the line as a node in its heading, so the parts read as one route.
  • The stretch's numbers are its first line, in bold, as before #780: "11.8 km · 38 min · ↑71 m". Under them, small: how it's drawn, with its icon, and the waypoints it folds.
  • How it's drawn on every stretch, "Follow paths" too, so a stretch along paths doesn't look like one with something missing; the ones that aren't along paths are green. The switch shows it only where it differs, as #780 does.
  • Said once: a part of one stretch has no numbers under its heading, as its stretch says them (PR 2 rebuilds the heading).
  • Waypoints: the points a stretch folds are "3 waypoints", never "shaping points". The list's other words that say "point" are in the plan.

What Parts changes (the third round)

  • Each sport has its colour: its part of the line and its icon at the head of the part are in the sport's colour, the one the map and the profile's strip already use for it (Colour by Sport); in dark mode a lighter one, for 3:1 on the dark glass. A route of one sport keeps the grey line.
  • Every part's heading looks the same, the first too: the sport's icon in a ring of its colour, on the line in the marks' column, then its name and its two ends. (Line had the ring only from the second part on, and the first heading looked like something else.)
  • The line breaks where the sport changes: it goes into the changeover point in one sport's colour, then dots lead past the rule between the parts to the next sport's icon. The changeover's own icon is in the next sport's colour too. Colour is never alone: the heading has the sport's icon and name, the point its tag.
  • A straight line is dashed, as on the map, and says so in words; a stretch that isn't along paths has its word in the text colour, bolder, not the accent's green.
  • Folded waypoints show on the line: a bead for each, three at most, and the words say which: "waypoints 1–5". The marks are numbered as on the map, so the point after five folded waypoints is 6.

What Parts 2 changes (the fourth round)

  • A wait is a part of its own: a changeover, a stop or a night has its own row, dotted on the line with its icon (a clock, a pause, a moon) and its time: "Changeover · 5 min", "Stop · 20 min", "Night · go on at 08:00". A tap opens its point's card there. The point's row no longer says "Hike from here": the next part's heading does. A changeover and a night sit between two parts; a stop stays in its part, which goes on in its colour after it.
  • Check's warnings on each stretch, as Check says them, with its shapes and colours: "2 to read", "1 not checked", and with the switch "1 worth a look" too. The gear left at a changeover is on its wait ("Your bike stays here").
  • Waypoint n for a waypoint with no name of its own, in place of Via n.
  • The word for a straight stretch is a switch: Straight line, Off paths or Freehand. It's the toolbar's mode too.
  • On a desktop the stretch's tooltip (the app's own) says where it starts and ends along the route and when you're there: the switch Hover shows one.

What Parts 3 changes (the fifth round)

  • Every part says when it starts: under the heading of a part after a changeover or a night, when you set off and the weather then ("Go on at 10:06 · 2° · 3 m/s"), as the start's row says it for the first part. The wait says only how long it lasts ("Changeover · 5 min", "Night · 13 h").

What Parts 4 changes (the sixth round)

  • Every part starts with a row: a part after a changeover or a night starts with its first point's row, the point where the last part ended, as the route starts with its start's row: its mark, its name, and when you set off and the weather then ("Go on at 08:00 · 4° · 2 m/s · Waypoint 4"). The point is in the list twice, as you get there and as you set off, as a timetable has it.
  • A wait says its times: "Changeover · 5 min (10:01–10:06)", "Stop · 15 min (11:05–11:20)", "Night · 13 h (19:00–08:00)", read at a glance. A night has sunset and sunrise under it ("Sunset 16:12 · sunrise 07:58"), the times Go and Check already work out (no service).
  • Freehand for a stretch Polku doesn't route (chosen on 2026-11-04), beside Follow paths.

What Parts 5 changes (the seventh round)

  • A point's line, at a glance: "Arrive 10:27", "Leave 10:06", "Leave now", then the weather then as icons, the sky and the wind ("☁ 2° ≋ 3 m/s"), in place of "There at 10:27 · 2° · 3 m/s · Waypoint 9". Start, End and Waypoint n leave the line, since the mark says them; a screen reader still hears them.
  • A wait's times under its name, in the grey line, as a point's are: "Changeover · 5 min" over "10:01–10:06". A night has sunset and sunrise with their own icons ("19:00–08:00 ↓ 16:12 ↑ 07:58"); the two icons are new, drawn in the app's icons' stroke. The switch Sun times shows the app's moon and sun in their place; the moon is also a clear night's sky on a point's line and the night's own icon, so there it means three things.
  • A stretch's folded waypoints are a count by its ⌄ ("5 ⌄") and beads on the line; their numbers are in the button's label, not the stretch's words.
  • A part's heading is its sport (and its day), its numbers under it where it has more than one stretch: its first and last rows already name its two ends.
  • On a desktop the tooltips of a stretch and of a point have the weather there too, with the distance and the time: the switch Hover shows a stretch's (Row 2) or a point's.

What Parts 6 changes (the eighth round)

  • Every part has its totals beside its name, in the text colour, so they read as the whole part's: "Trail run · 984 m · 9 min · ↑39 m", the parts of one stretch too. A route of one part has none there: the route's numbers above say them (#764).
  • A route of one sport looks like the others: its heading is on the line, the sport's icon in a ring of its colour, and the line in its colour; "MTB · Start → End" beside Reorder is gone.
  • Sunset and sunrise with the horizon icons, chosen on 2026-11-04.

What Parts 7 changes (the ninth round)

  • A route of one part has its totals in its heading too, as every part has, though the route's numbers above say them: one style everywhere, chosen on 2026-11-04 over saying a number once (#764).

Left out

  • The map, the toolbar and the profile are stand-ins; the numbers are made up for a 5.8 km route.
  • The cards and the popup (#760), Reorder's own view, a night's row and a long route's folding (#350): not drawn.
  • Line keeps main's foot, How you go and Your speed, and the bottom dock's one column: the earlier PRs of the plan change those. Its unfolded waypoints aren't drawn; on the line they're marks like the others.
  • The first inline list (2026-10-13 to 2026-10-25) had a name field, five tools, the leg's settings and Depart on every row; its lessons are in the plan.
