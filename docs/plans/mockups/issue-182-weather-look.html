Weather on the map: rain, cloud, thunder, snow

For #182: how the weather layers could look. Today is main as it is. A lets the motion say how hard it rains, with light colours under it. B lets the colours say it, in clear steps, with less motion. Both add Clouds, Thunder and the Towns' weather. Thunder keeps the lightning that was seen apart from the thunder the forecast expects. Snow shows the snowfall and the snow on the ground together. The map's hours are the weather line of the weather card's plan (step 6b): its pill (“Rain ▾”) opens the layers' menu, and its strip starts with the radar's last 90 minutes. A legend sits over the map while a layer is on. Timeline today on the Bar switch keeps the old timeline to compare. The weather is made up, placed in the middle of what the map shows (Kauppi's MTB route in Tampere, and the region around it); the maps are main's.

What each look does

  • Rain, A: drizzle is a few short, slow, faint streaks; rain is today's streaks; heavy rain is long, thick and dense, so the three read apart in motion. The colour under it stays light (at most 38 % covering, today 85 %), so trails, words and the route stay readable.
  • Rain, B: the colour in steps (drizzle, rain, heavy, very heavy), each with its edge drawn, like a radar map. The streaks are half as many and fainter.
  • Cloud, A: soft shadows drifting with the wind, darker where the cover is thicker. B: a white veil, drifting the same way. On the night map both turn into a faint light veil.
  • Thunder, A: each lightning strike as a small bolt, by its age: under 10 min, 10–30 min and 30–60 min, in size and colour, and the words in the legend. Where the forecast expects thunder, an amber hatched area. B: strikes as dots by age, and the forecast's thunder as a warm glow. A new batch of strikes flashes once.
  • Snow, A: the snowfall in violet, so it reads apart from the snow on the ground, whose cool wash stays light; bare ground stays clear. B: the depth in steps (1, 10, 25, 50 cm) with their edges. In every look FMI's measured depths sit on top as small labels (“24”), and the legend says which are measured.
  • The weather line (step 6b): ▷, the sky, the temperature, the hour with the wind (gust) and rain, the layer's pill, ⌃ and ✕, over the strip. The pill names the layer that's on (“Rain +1” for two) and opens the menu: Rain and snowfall, Clouds, Thunder, Snow on the ground, Wind, Temperature, and the Towns' weather. On a phone the line takes the sheet's place; on a desktop it opens above the toolbar's right end.
  • The legend sits over the map, top left, while a layer is on, as yr's app does: each layer's colours in words, the nearest lightning (“6 km, 4 min ago”), a warning when FMI has one for the area, and what's missing.
  • The radar's last 90 minutes start the strip (“Radar”): drag back to see where the rain came from. Only the rain has a past: clouds, thunder and snow depth start at Now, and the legend says so. Ahead of Now the forecast goes on; a radar nowcast for the next 90 minutes is a source still to check.
  • The towns' weather, zoomed out: the sky, the temperature and the wind at towns, as yr's map shows them, at the line's hour. Only at a region's scale; near the route the route's own weather is the line's reading.
  • Moving the time: lightning is seen, not forecast, so it shows at Now only (its last hour); at a later hour the forecast's thunder is all there is, and the legend says so.
  • When a source fails: the legend says which (“Lightning couldn't be loaded”); the map never looks calm because a source is missing.
  • Reduced motion: the rain and snow are drawn still, the clouds don't drift and nothing flashes. Today the particles move in reduced motion too.

What isn't decided here

  • Which forecast says thunder: Open-Meteo's weather code (95–99), its CAPE, or both; lightning from FMI's open data (flashes with their time and place).
  • Whether thunder near the route belongs in Heads-up: not in #182, an idea of its own if it's wanted.
  • Warnings on the map as areas, and in the weather card: the warning's row here is a placeholder for that issue's design.
  • Where the towns' symbols come from: one forecast call per town would burst Open-Meteo, so they come from the map's own weather grid (the nearest cell), or not at all.
  • The motion is decided with /animate when it's built, and checked with /review-animations before its PR opens.
