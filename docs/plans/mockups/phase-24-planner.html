A calmer route planner (#233)

2026-10-17. Builds on phase 24's review and the choices made from its first mock-up (B: the tools float, a one-row peek, only the selected waypoint's buttons). The reasons, the plan to build it and what Done means are in docs/plans/issue-233-calmer-planner.md. Not wired to the app: the map behind each frame is a screenshot of Polku with its controls hidden.

Every frame can be tapped and keeps its own state; Reset starts it again. Tap the map to add points, try the toolbox, the round trip, Done and Edit, More, Check and the waypoint list. The switches at the bottom apply to every frame: the worst case (long Finnish names, 52 points, long numbers, many warnings), language (rough Finnish and Swedish, only to test the room words take) and theme.

Final, 2026-10-17: the layout and the interactions here are decided. What's drawn simply on purpose (the profile, Climbs, On the route, On the map, Go's time and the weather along the route, and parts of the leg editor) stays exactly as Polku has it today: the mock-up shows where things go, not everything in them. The plan lists what changes, what moves and what stays as it is.

What's decided

  1. Drawing and routing told apart. The toolbox holds how the next leg is drawn (Follow paths, Straight line, Over water), undo, redo and More. The route style (Balanced, Flowy trails…) is a setting of the whole route: it shows under Follow paths, because it's what Follow paths prefers, and has its own labelled group of chips. Phones: one drop-down. Desktops: a labelled toolbar on the panel's edge, with tooltips and keys, and the route's actions at its end: Reverse, Back to start, Show the whole route, More, Done.
  2. Round trips in the peek after the first point: presets one tap away, any distance or time, then a green Make 3 loops. Nothing on the map; a tap on the map still adds the next point.
  3. More (⋯) for the whole route, at the end of the toolbox and the toolbar: take it along, change the whole route, and Clear apart. The route's summary shows the route.
  4. Done means done editing. The route stays open to read, check and take along (the route's view); Edit goes back, in the same place. ✕ puts it away; nothing is lost either way. Phones: Done at the summary's right, at the thumb; desktops: at the toolbar's end.
  5. Show the whole route is always there while a route is on the map: in the map's column under Locate on phones, in the toolbar on desktops (and F).
  6. Check by how serious and how sure: Read before you go, Worth a look, Not checked, and where each comes from.
  7. Waypoints: only the selected one has buttons, no Remove on the others; finding, stepping, moving and undoing in lists of 50 and more.

0 · Try it

The whole way through, on a phone and a desktop: tap the map for a first point, try a round trip or tap again for a route, change the drawing mode, press Done, open More, swipe… (here: press the handle) for the sheet's sizes, and look at Check.

1 · The toolbox: how the next leg is drawn

Decided: one drop-down on phones; the labelled toolbar with tooltips on desktops. Two different ideas, two places. The drawing mode is a tool: it decides how the next leg you draw is made, and leaves the drawn legs alone (a leg's own mode is changed by tapping it on the map, as today). The route style is a setting: it decides which ways Follow paths prefers, for the whole route, and changing it routes the route again. So the mode is the button, and the style is its second line, shown only for the modes it applies to; in the menu the modes are a list with what each does, and the styles a set of chips under "Route style · the whole route".

  • Phones: one button with the mode's icon and name (never an icon alone), undo, redo and More (⋯), floating at the bottom left above the sheet. Where it can't clear the map's buttons (narrow phones, long words, 200 % zoom) or with the sheet raised high, it docks into the sheet as its first row instead. In the route's view only More stays.
  • Desktops: the toolbar hangs off the panel's edge, one surface with it, so it reads as part of planning, not another floating panel. The three modes with their names, the style as its own labelled control, undo and redo; then the route's actions: Reverse, Back to start and Show the whole route as icons with tooltips; then More and Done. Tooltips give the key and one sentence; keyboard focus shows them too. With less room, Reverse and Back to start fold into More (they're there anyway), then the style's label goes. In the route's view the toolbar keeps Show the whole route, More and Edit.
  • States: the mode in use is filled and ticked, not only coloured; undo and redo show from the first change and are disabled (not hidden) when there's nothing to undo or redo; at the start there's nothing to show but the mode.

2 · Round trips, in the peek

After the first point the peek says what to do next (tap the map for the next point) and, under it, offers a round trip from here: the sport's lengths, and Other. A tap on a length doesn't make loops yet: it opens the round trip with that length chosen and a green Make 3 loops, so a thumb that misses the map never starts three routing calls. Any distance or time is − / + or typed; the direction and the start are there too, as today. A tap on the map still adds the next point while the round trip is closed, and moves the start while it's open (and does nothing while the loops are being made, #109).

3 · More (⋯), for the whole route

Decided: More (⋯), familiar, compact, and not tied to one kind of action. It's the last button of the toolbox on phones (beside the drawing tools, not in the route's summary) and of the toolbar on desktops; in the route's view it stays there, the drawing tools gone. Grouped by what it's for: take it along (a share link, a GPX file, the map saved for no signal), the whole route (a round trip from the start, reverse, back to start, a file to plan on), and Clear route apart, in red, after a gap, with Undo. Its head says the route is kept on this device. What's also somewhere else, on purpose: Take it along in Go, Reverse and Back to start in Route's tools (and the desktop toolbar), Import at the start. Moving the panel stays with the panel (22b).

4 · Done, the route's view, and coming back

Finishing isn't one thing. Done ends editing: the toolbox goes, a tap on the map shows what's there instead of adding a point, and the route stays open to read, check and take along. That's the route's view: the route's name, its numbers, and Edit to go back, where Done was (the summary's right on phones, the toolbar's end on desktops); More stays. ✕ puts the route away (it stays on the map; Plan brings it back as it was). Nothing is lost by any of them: the route is kept on this device all along, and undo outlives Done.

5 · Check: how serious, and how sure

A count alone doesn't say whether to worry. Check sorts what's already found into three groups, each with its own icon, word and shape (never colour alone): Read before you go (what can hurt, stop you or turn you back: shipping lanes, rough water, ice, the dark, road works, no access), Worth a look (sections to know about: steep, technical, carries, steps, ways marked private, trail details), and Not checked (a source that didn't answer, an estimate standing in). Each item says where it comes from: live, from the map (may be wrong), an estimate, or calculated. The end of Check says what was looked at, and that nothing listed doesn't mean open, safe or allowed. The tab's badge and a row in the peek follow the most serious group. Nothing new is checked: these are today's Heads-up items, sorted.

6 · Waypoints, and long routes

Only the selected point has buttons: its name, its stop, the leg after it (today's leg editor: its sport, how it's drawn and its style, each the route's unless set here), move earlier or later or to a number, and Remove, last and apart. The buttons are small to look at and 44 px to touch. The other rows are one button each: tap to select. There's no Remove on them, so a tap meant for a row never deletes. For long routes: a search, Key points (start, finish, stops, changes of sport, named points, legs of their own) or All, a bar that keeps the selected point in sight with previous and next, and Undo for every change. The map and the list select together: a tap on a marker selects its row.
