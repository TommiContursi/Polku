What changes

  • The layers are buttons: Rain, Clouds, Thunder, Wind, Temperature and Snow depth, each with its icon and the name it has today. A tap turns it on or off; any of them at once, as the menu's ticks today. On, a button has the app's chosen-chip look: a green tint, a 2 px green border and a green icon.
  • Wind lines: while Temperature is on, the two share one colour field, so the map colours by temperature and the wind shows only its lines. The Wind button then says Wind lines. It keeps the room of its longer name, so nothing moves when Temperature is switched.
  • ⋯ beside ✕ holds Weather at places and Effects, with the sources' note, in the app's menu: the rest of today's menu.
  • On a desktop (Buttons on a row of their own) the title bar stays as it is, with Timeline and its room to grab, and the buttons take a row under it. In a narrow window they wrap. Buttons on the title bar is the sketch's way: they take the title bar, the word Timeline goes (its clock stays as the handle to drag), and the window opens 104 px wider so the six fit.
  • On a phone both versions are the same: the buttons take the pill's place in the row that's there, so the timeline is no taller. They scroll sideways, and fade at the edge where there's more.
  • A tooltip on a desktop gives each button the line it has in the menu today.

What stays as it is

  • The window, where it opens, dragging it by its title bar and resizing it by its grip (a desktop), the strip, the slider, Play, the time, the reading and the note under them, and the legend over the map.

Questions for you

  • A phone at 360 px shows about two and a half buttons; the rest are a swipe away, and the legend over the map names what's on. Today's pill counts them ("Rain +2"). Is the sideways row enough? Try All six on a phone.
  • Weather at places behind ⋯ shows its state on the map only, and ⋯ is otherwise kept for settings (#690). Keep it there, or make it a seventh button?
  • Snow depth adds a line to the reading, so the timeline grows 12 px (16 on a phone) and the buttons move up under the finger. Today it happens behind the open menu. Leave it, or keep the line's room always (16 px on every phone)?

Left out of the mock-up

  • The map's layers are drawn by hand, roughly; the app draws them from the forecast. Weather at places puts no marks on this map.
  • The radar's past isn't loaded: its scans are the app's. The route's band on the strip is part 2's.
  • Today's menu shows a line under each layer's name. With buttons, a desktop has it as the tooltip; a phone has the legend over the map.
