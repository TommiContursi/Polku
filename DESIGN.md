# Design System: Polku

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Map Comes First"**

The map fills the screen and always has the stage. Everything else floats over it on glass, so the map shows through: a reading glass for what you read (the top bar, the planner, the layer drawer, the panels, the card at a point on a desktop) and a whiter glass for the map's buttons. The menus, popups and dialogs are solid. On a phone the planner becomes a sheet from the bottom, on a desktop a panel at the side. Nothing covers the map that the moment doesn't need, and what's covered can always be brought back in one step.

The components are calm and sure-footed. Surfaces are quiet and nearly neutral, a cool green-grey, so the map's colours and the magenta route line carry the colour. There is one clear main action at a time, in forest green. Targets are at least 44 px where a finger uses them, and words say what happens ("Make 3 loops", "Back to start") rather than leaning on icons alone. The look is built to be legible outdoors: in bright sun, with one thumb, at a glance.

This file holds the tokens: colours, type, shapes, spacing, depth and motion. **The controls themselves are listed in [docs/DEVELOPING.md, "Shared controls"](docs/DEVELOPING.md#shared-controls)**, which is the one list of them; a new screen reuses those for the same jobs. The values here are the ones on `:root` in `css/app.css`, named by what they're for, which stays the source: when the two differ, the CSS is right and this file is out of date. `python3 tools/smoke.py values` fails when a colour in the frontmatter isn't the CSS's, in either theme. **Where a rule below isn't yet true everywhere, it says so.** [The design-system plan](docs/plans/design-system.md) (#382) is how it becomes true; until then, a new screen follows the rule, not the exception.

**Key Characteristics:**
- The map first; controls on glass over it, solid when the person asks for more contrast or less transparency.
- Quiet neutral surfaces, one green accent, the route in magenta.
- One main action per step, in words.
- 44 px targets for touch, numbers in tabular figures.
- Light and dark from the same tokens, following the person's setting.

## Colors

A quiet, cool green-grey interface with one forest-green accent, so that the map and the route line hold the colour. The names below are the CSS variables in `css/app.css` (`--accent`, `--route`…); dark mode redefines the same variables, listed in the frontmatter with a `dark-` prefix.

### Primary
- **Accent (forest green)**: links, the chosen chip's border, the chosen switch's icon, ticks and the active state of map buttons. In dark mode a lighter green, so it still reads on dark surfaces.
- **Focus (green)**: the focus ring, on every control: the accent's green in light mode and a lighter one in dark.
- **Accent-bg (forest green)**: the background behind white text and icons, such as the main action (`btn primary`), **Plan**, the sport pill and pressed buttons. Light mode uses the accent itself; dark mode a darker green, because white on the lighter dark-mode accent is only 2.7:1, and on this one 5.3:1 (#239).
- **Accent-2 (lighter green)** and **Accent-soft (pale green tint)**: hover and the soft fill behind a chosen item.

### Secondary
- **Route (magenta)**: the route line on the map and its glow. It is the one strong colour on the screen and is kept for the route. A navigation magenta, apart from every red the map draws (the Trail map's S3 trails and no-bike ticks, LIPAS's hiking routes), and from them with colour blindness too (#547).
- **Route-text (a darker magenta, a pink in dark mode)**: words in the route's colour, such as the Weather button's time ahead and the timeline's ⚑, at 4.5:1 on either glass over any map (#559). The route's own colour is under that on the glass.
- **The route's legs**: over water deep blue (`--route-water`), with the same glow, and on the light maps a dark edge in place of the white one, which the sea's pale blue loses (#611); the map's key says so ("Over water"); a straight line darker magenta and dashed (`--route-line`); a leg being routed grey and dotted (`--route-loading`). They sit on the map, so they're the same in both themes.
- **Colour by** in the planner can recolour the route line on the map (with its **On map** switch) by grade, uphill difficulty, surface, kind of way, trail width, wind, temperature or rain; with any of them the plain magenta is replaced along the whole line. Each scale's colours are values too (`--diff-s0` … `--diff-s6`, `--grade-…`, `--surf-…`, `--sport-…`, `--way-…`, `--trail-width-…`, `--trail-vis-…`, `--temp-…`, `--rain-…`, `--wind-…`), while its words and order stay in its table in `js/config.js`, `js/chart.js` or `js/weather.js`. The weather map's own colours are still in `js/wxmap.js`. How the scales read, for colour blindness too, is in the design-system plan's §2.
- **In the JavaScript**, the route's colours, the warning colours and the scales come from the CSS: `fromCss()` in `js/palette.js` reads them at start and again when the theme changes, and the map and the planner draw with them again.
- **You (blue)**: the location dot and its accuracy circle (`--you-soft`). Blue is kept for "where you are" and for water.

### Tertiary
- **Good (green), Ok (amber), Warn (orange), Bad (red)**: states and warnings, such as Heads-up rows, difficulty and conditions, as lines, bars, dots and icons. Never the only way something is said: each comes with words or an icon. Bad is Danger's red, so danger has one red. Each has a lighter version in dark mode, so red words such as Remove and Delete read there too (#370).
- **Good-text, Ok-text, Warn-text, Bad-text**: the same four for words and pills, at least 6.2:1 in both themes: the condition pills, a warning on a layer's tile, and Check's count (warn-text behind it, the surface's colour on it, so it reads in both themes).
- **Ink (black)**: words on one of the map's or a scale's colours, where white doesn't read on it: a climb's badge, a loop's letter. `darkWords()` in `js/palette.js` picks black or white, whichever reads, at 4.58:1 or more. The same in both themes, since it sits on the colour.
- **Wx-text (blue)**: the weather's blue, for words: an active weather layer's chip in Layers. Lighter in dark mode.
- **Danger (red), Look (amber), Unk (blue-grey)**, each with a soft version for the box behind it: Check's three groups (25d), Read before you go, Worth a look and Not checked. Each has its shape too: a triangle, an eye, a dashed "?". At least 5.4:1 on its box and on the page, in both themes. Danger's box is solid in dark mode too, so it doesn't take the glass's tint. 25d's Journey C is the first to use them.

### Neutral
- **Glass-read (white at 72 %; near-black at 73 % in dark mode)**: the reading glass, for what you read over the map: the top bar with ⚙, the zoom hint, the planner, the timeline, the toolbox and the desktop's toolbar, the layer drawer, the panels, the card at a point on a desktop (at the point, or beside the panel) and a track's card.
- **Glass-control (white at 84 %; near-black at 84 %)**: the whiter glass of the map's buttons: the compass, Locate, zoom, 3D, Weather, Layers when open and the 3D view's controls, so they read as one group and stay close to one tint over a busy map.
- **Muted-glass, accent-glass and bad-glass**: a darker grey, green and red on the glass (lighter in dark mode), so words keep 4.5:1 and icons 3:1 over the Trail map, Maastokartta and the aerial photos (`tools/smoke.py glass`). Every glass surface sets its grey, green and red to these, so its lines, borders and rings take them too; those aren't measured over the map yet.
- **Surface-solid (white)**: menus, popups, dialogs, inputs and buttons that need an opaque background, and every glass when the person asks for more contrast or less transparency.
- **Surface-2 (pale grey-green)**: hover on rows and buttons, and recessed areas.
- **Seg-on**: a switch's chosen segment: white, and in dark mode a grey lighter than its track, so it never reads as a gap.
- **Text (near-black green)** and **Muted (grey-green)**: body text and secondary text, such as hints, units and section labels.
- **Line (text at 14 %)**: borders and dividers.
- **For more contrast**: **Muted-hc**, a grey closer to the text; **Line-hc**, lines at 35 %; and the rim, **Hc-border** outside and **Hc-inner** inside, 3:1 against any map.
- **Tile-rim**: the Layers button's light inner rim, over its picture.
- **Grip (grey)**: every grip and handle, at least 3:1 on its surface. Today the grips are still a paler grey (#382's controls and touch PRs).
- **Page (pale grey-green)**: the page behind the map, seen only before the map draws.
- **Chart ink and chart grid**: the profile's words and lines, drawn on its canvas.
- The map's own colours (land, water, parks, labels) are in `js/style.js`, one palette per map style. The sports' own line colours are `--sport-…` (`SPORT_COLORS` in `js/config.js`).

### Named Rules
**The Map Holds the Colour Rule.** Interface surfaces stay neutral. Magenta belongs to the route, red to danger, blue to water and to where you are; the accent marks what's chosen or the one main action, nothing decorative. **Plan** is green, like every other main action. Today one thing breaks it, decided on 2026-10-22 (#382): **Colour by**'s wind scale draws headwinds in red, and goes orange to blue in the map and scales PR.

**The Never by Colour Alone Rule.** Every colour that means something (a warning, a difficulty, a chosen state) is also said in words, a tick, an icon or a shape (PRODUCT.md, WCAG 2.2 AA).

## Typography

**Body Font:** the system's interface font (`system-ui`, San Francisco on Apple devices, Segoe UI on Windows, Roboto on Android). Inter is named first in the stack but is not loaded, so it shows only where it's installed.

**Character:** one familiar sans-serif in a few weights, compact on a desktop and readable on a phone; bold carries the hierarchy, not size jumps.

### Hierarchy

These are the nine sizes, values on `:root` named by what they're for (`--font-glance` … `--font-small`). They're in `rem`, so the browser's text size enlarges them. A control takes its value when it's next touched (the design-system plan's §3), so today most rules still type their size in px: 18 sizes between 9 and 22 px, half sizes included (11.5, 12.5, 13.5, 14.5, 16.5).

- **Glance** (700, 34 px, `--font-glance`, tabular figures): a number read while moving, in `--text` on `--surface-solid`, never on the glass. Phase 23 is the first to use it.
- **Numeral** (700, 22 px, `--font-numeral`, line-height 1, tabular figures): the big numbers, such as the time in the timeline and the round trip's length.
- **Number** (700, 19 px, `--font-number`, tabular figures): a card's numbers.
- **Title** (700, 17 px, `--font-title`): a step's title, such as "Round trip from your start", a card's name and the map's cards' titles (`insp-title`), and the main action's words (`btn primary big`). On a desktop's card at its spot it's 15 px.
- **Field** (400, 16 px, `--font-field`): what you type, on phones, so iOS doesn't zoom the page. A panel's or drawer's heading is 16 px too today.
- **Row** (600, 15 px, `--font-row`): the words of a row you tap, such as a sport, a point's action or a way to begin. Some other controls still use 650, which the system fonts draw as 600 or 700.
- **Body** (400, 14 px, `--font-body`, line-height 1.4): the page's base size.
- **Control** (600, 13 px, `--font-control`): button labels on a desktop, and a row's second line.
- **Small** (600, 12 px, `--font-small`): hints, values and lists; the most used size in the panels. Reading text is never smaller (the Small on Purpose Rule below); today some still is (#235).
- **Small heading** (700, 12 px, `--font-small`, letter-spacing 0.06em, uppercase, in `--muted`): every section label, such as a menu's group, a layer group, a planner section or a search result group. One rule in `css/app.css` gives them all their look.

The weights are `--weight-regular` (400), `--weight-name` (600) and `--weight-strong` (700). On a desktop's card at the point (`.card-float`) the title, the numbers and the rows are one step smaller (15, 17 and 14 px), set once on the card.

### Named Rules
**The Tabular Numbers Rule.** Numbers that change or line up (times, distances, the timeline) use tabular figures, so they don't jump as they update.

**The Room for Finnish Rule.** Names, headings and labels wrap rather than run out of their box (`overflow-wrap: anywhere`), because Finnish and Swedish are coming (phase 18) and their words are long. A button's words wrap inside it: buttons have a least height, never a fixed one (#373). On a narrow card (under about 220 px, as at 200 %), a row's value goes under its name (#384).

**The Small on Purpose Rule.** Polku is a map, and some text is small so the map has the room: the scale bar, the credits, the map's own labels, chart axes, units beside a number, a word under an icon, a number inside a mark. Those may go down to 10 px. Text you read to decide or act (names, values, actions, warnings, a row's line) is 12 px or more. Small or not, text passes 4.5:1 and grows with the browser's text size.

## Layout

The map is fixed behind everything (`#map`, inset 0). Over it:
- **The top bar** sits 12 px from the top and left edges, up to 730 px wide.
- **The map's buttons** stand at the right edge.
- **The planner** is a side panel on a desktop and a sheet from the bottom on a phone, with a peek, a middle and a full height. The layer drawer is a card beside the Layers button on a desktop, a sheet on a phone.

The switch is at **720 px**: up to 720 px wide is the phone layout, 721 px and up the desktop one; there's a wider step at 900 px, and short screens (under 500 px or 760 px high) get a more compact sheet. Touch is decided by the pointer, not the width: `pointer: coarse` grows targets to 44 px, and hover styles apply only where `hover: hover`.

Spacing is a tight rhythm: 6 and 8 px between items in a row, 10 to 14 px of padding inside cards, 12 px from the screen's edges. Its values are 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16 and 24 px (`--space-2` … `--space-24`), for new work and for a control when it moves onto the values. Targets are 44 px by touch at any width and 36 px with a mouse, 8 px apart (#380): `--target` is 36 px, and 44 px under `pointer: coarse`. A row is `--row-h` (50 px; 36 px on a desktop's card) and a card's mark `--mark` (34 px; 28 px). A target is the area that answers a tap, not the size it looks: a control can be small to look at, with its touch area grown around it. Today many controls are still smaller (#235).

## Elevation & Depth

Depth comes from floating over the map: what floats is glass with one soft shadow, and the map moves under it. The reading glass blurs what's under it by 32 px and lifts its colours by 5 % (`--glass-read-filter`: `blur(32px) saturate(105%)`), so a sheet stays one quiet tone over any map and its trails still show; the map's buttons blur by 24 px (`--glass-control-filter`: `blur(24px) saturate(105%)`). One shadow token (`--shadow`) carries most of what floats; nothing stacks shadows to make a hierarchy. There are four shadow values; today about 38 other shadows are still typed in place in `css/app.css`, and they move onto the four when they're next touched (#382).

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Marker** (`--shadow-marker`: `0 1px 4px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.35)`, the same in both themes): a marker on the map.
- **Float** (`--shadow`: `0 8px 28px rgba(16, 24, 20, 0.18), 0 1px 3px rgba(16, 24, 20, 0.1)`; `0 8px 28px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.35), 0 1px 3px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.25)` in dark mode): every card, sheet, menu and map button over the map.
- **Edge** (`--edge`: `inset 0 0 0 1px` white at 70 %, `inset 0 1px 0` white at 95 % and `0 0 0 1px rgba(16, 24, 20, 0.26)`; in dark mode white at 34 % and 42 % inside and black at 70 % outside): every glass surface's two-tone edge, light inside and soft outside, beside the float shadow. In more contrast it's the 3:1 rim.
- **Sheet edge** (`--shadow-sheet`: `0 -6px 28px rgba(16, 24, 20, 0.2)`; black at 0.5 in dark mode): the phone's bottom sheet and the panels' edge, casting upwards.
- **Dialog** (`--shadow-dialog`: `0 24px 60px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.35)`, the same in both themes): a dialog over everything.
- **Ring** (`inset 0 0 0 2px` in the accent or the route colour): a chosen or open state, added to the float shadow.

### Named Rules
**The One Float Rule.** Everything over the map uses the same `--shadow`. A chosen state adds a ring, never a bigger shadow.

**The Edge Rule.** Every glass surface has the edge with its shadow (`box-shadow: var(--shadow), var(--edge)`). A ring for a chosen or open state adds to it.

**The Clear Glass Rule.** When the person asks for reduced transparency (`prefers-reduced-transparency`) or more contrast (`prefers-contrast: more`, or Settings' **High contrast**), every glass turns solid (`--surface-solid`, no blur). More contrast also takes the darker grey, heavier lines and the 3:1 rim (`data-contrast` on `<html>`, set as `data-theme` is).

## Shapes

Gently rounded, never sharp. The corners are values: `--radius-small` (6 px) for small things such as swatches and inputs, `--radius-control` (10 px) for a control, `--radius` (14 px) for cards, panels and tiles, `--radius-sheet` (20 px) for the top of a phone's sheet, `--radius-pill` (999 px) for chips, the sport pill and switches, and `--radius-round` (50 %) for icon-only map buttons and dots. Today buttons and rows are still 10 to 12 px. The planner's sheet floats 8 px in from the screen's edges on a phone, with 18 px corners all round; the layer drawer's and the panels' sheets have 20 px corners at the top only. Borders are a single 1 px line in `--line`, used sparingly.

## Components

The controls, with their classes and where each comes from, are in **[docs/DEVELOPING.md, "Shared controls"](docs/DEVELOPING.md#shared-controls)**. That table is the one list: this file doesn't repeat it. A new screen uses those controls for the same jobs, a mock-up copies their CSS from `css/app.css`, and a new control is proposed to the user first and then joins that table.

On the glass:
- **The Layers button**: its map picture with a light inner rim (`--tile-rim`) and the glass's soft outer line; its word on a dark band that's 4.5:1 or more over a white picture.
- **An undo that can't undo** on the toolbox: the glass's grey at 70 %, readable and still dimmer than redo.
- **The logo on a phone**: the green mark alone, 44 px, with no pill round it. On a desktop its pill's corners are concentric with the mark's.

What every control shares:
- **Focus:** one 2 px outline in `--focus`, 2 px out, set once for every button, link, field and focusable element (`:where(…):focus-visible`); inside a row of a list or a menu's item (#385).
- **Hover:** `--surface-2` behind, only with a mouse. Today 38 of 57 hover rules also apply on touch screens, where they stay after a tap (#208), and the hover looks vary (`--surface-2`, `--surface-solid`, `--accent-soft`, `--accent-bg`).
- **Pressed:** buttons, chips, tiles and switches, the map's buttons and the top bar's too, shrink to 97 % for `--t-press` while held, with no grey flash; rows keep their grey (`--surface-2`). With reduced motion, nothing moves.
- **Chosen:** never colour alone (#356). A chip: a green tint, a 2 px green border, black words. A switch: white, black words, a green icon or tick. A tile: a green border, icon and tick, black words. A row: a green tint and a tick. A switch that's off has a `--muted` edge, at least 3:1.
- **Motion:** see [Motion](#motion) below: the timings, the curves and when things move.

## Motion

Polku's motion follows Emil Kowalski's design engineering ([Great animations](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations), [7 practical animation tips](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips)) and the skills built on it: `/animate` decides each motion and `/review-animations` checks it. The aim is motion nearly everywhere it helps: every press answers, and every change that means something moves. What people do many times a minute stays instant. Where a rule isn't yet true everywhere, #540's Motion 1, 2 and 3 make it true.

- **Timings:** `--t-press` (120 ms) for a press and hover, `--t-open` (180 ms) for cards and menus and `--t-sheet` (260 ms) for the sheet and the panel; closing takes the next shorter one. Interface motion stays under 300 ms. Today hover and press take 120 to 150 ms in places, typed in place.
- **Curves:** entering and leaving ease out on `--ease-out` (`cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`), and the sheet on `--ease-drawer` (`cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)`); never ease-in. What moves with a finger uses the sheet's spring (`js/sheet.js`).
- **Whether it moves:** never for an action started from the keyboard. What people see tens or hundreds of times a day (typing, undo, adding points, scrubbing) gets no motion, and hover effects come only with a mouse (`@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`). First paint and page load never animate, nor does a change of light, dark or contrast: the new look is drawn everywhere at once.
- **Press:** buttons, chips, tiles and switches shrink to 97 % while held (Pressed, under Components).
- **Entering:** nothing enters from `scale(0)`: from 0.9 or more (tooltips 0.97), with opacity. Menus, popovers and tooltips grow from their trigger (`transform-origin` at the trigger; dialogs stay centred) and leave the way they came, faster; a menu that opens in the place of one still leaving comes at once, and one with no room beside its trigger, a sheet of its own, only fades.
- **Tooltips:** a delay before the first one; once one is open, the next opens at once, with no animation.
- **Interrupting:** a move starts from where things are and can be caught mid-way: CSS transitions rather than keyframes.
- **Performance:** interface elements animate `transform` and `opacity` only (`filter: blur()` where needed), with CSS or WAAPI rather than `requestAnimationFrame`, so they stay smooth while the main thread is busy. The map's canvas effects (wind, rain, snow) draw each frame by nature. The one exception is a desktop card folding to its title row, which animates its height (120 ms to fold, 180 ms to open, `roll()` in `js/card.js`): `clip-path` would cut its shadow while it moves.
- **A crossfade** between two states that still feels off gets 2 px of blur.
- **The map:** colour and opacity changes use MapLibre's paint transitions; camera moves share one set of durations; no tweening of geometry.
- **Rebuilt content:** where only a state changes, the control shows its change through `settleControls()` (`js/ui.js`); nothing re-animates on a rebuild the person didn't cause. What opens in a rebuilt panel (a group's rows, a sport's speeds) fades in only on the tap that opened it.
- **Outdoors and battery:** nothing loops while idle unless it says something (busy), and it stops when done. A busy bar waits 300 ms before it shows, so a quick answer shows nothing, and it is never under something hidden.
- **Reduced motion:** opacity only: things fade in place instead of sliding, pulses stop, the map's camera moves jump, and the planner's list scrolls at once. A chosen look's colours still fade, since nothing moves, and nothing shrinks, grows or turns. A busy bar (routing) stays in place and pulses in opacity, since it says something.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** use the CSS variables (`var(--accent)`, `var(--glass-read)`, `var(--shadow)`), never their hex values, so dark mode follows. In the JavaScript, a colour that means something comes from the CSS through `fromCss()` in `js/palette.js`.
- **Do** keep targets at least 44 px for touch, and say every meaning in words as well as colour.
- **Do** put white text on `--accent-bg`, not on `--accent` (#239).
- **Do** reuse a control from DEVELOPING's "Shared controls" for a job it already does.

### Don't:
- **Don't** use red for anything but danger, magenta for anything but the route, or blue for anything but water and where you are.
- **Don't** add a second shadow, a heavier shadow or a new radius to make something stand out; use the ring, the accent or the words.
- **Don't** make a copy of an existing control under a new class name.
- **Don't** cover the map with anything the moment doesn't need.
