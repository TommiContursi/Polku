# Design System: Polku

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Map Comes First"**

The map fills the screen and always has the stage. Everything else floats over it on glass, so the map shows through: a reading glass for what you read (the top bar, the planner, the layer drawer, the panels, the card at a point on a desktop) and a whiter glass for the map's buttons. The menus, popups and dialogs are solid. On a phone the planner becomes a sheet from the bottom, on a desktop a panel at the side. Nothing covers the map that the moment doesn't need, and what's covered can always be brought back in one step.

The components are calm and sure-footed. Surfaces are quiet and nearly neutral, a cool green-grey, so the map's colours and the magenta route line carry the colour. There is one clear main action at a time, in forest green. Targets are at least 44 px where a finger uses them, and words say what happens ("Make 3 loops", "Back to start") rather than leaning on icons alone. The look is built to be legible outdoors: in bright sun, with one thumb, at a glance.

This file holds the tokens: colours, type, shapes, spacing, depth and motion. **The controls themselves are listed in [docs/DEVELOPING.md, "Shared controls"](docs/DEVELOPING.md#shared-controls)**, which is the one list of them; a new screen reuses those for the same jobs. The values here are the ones on `:root` in `css/app.css`, named by what they're for, which stays the source: when the two differ, the CSS is right and this file is out of date. `python3 tools/smoke.py values` fails when a colour in the frontmatter isn't the CSS's, in either theme. **Where a rule below isn't yet true everywhere, it says so.** [The design-system plan](docs/plans/design-system.md) (#382) is how it becomes true; until then, a new screen follows the rule, not the exception.

**Key Characteristics:**
- The map first; controls on glass over it, solid when the person asks for more contrast or less transparency.
- Quiet neutral surfaces, one green accent, the route in magenta.
- One main action per step, in words.
- 44 px targets for touch, numbers in tabular figures.
- Light and dark from the same tokens, following the person's setting.

## Colors

A quiet, cool green-grey interface with one forest-green accent, so that the map and the route line hold the colour. The names below are the CSS variables in `css/app.css` (`--accent`, `--route`…); dark mode redefines the same variables, listed in the frontmatter with a `dark-` prefix.

The Trail map keeps its pale terrain fills, with stronger road edges and flat building outlines so streets, trails and buildings can be read together. Minor and service roads widen modestly at intermediate zooms; cycling sports retain the warm unpaved-road emphasis. Other basemaps and 3D building colours keep their own appearance.

Cycling paths use surface-specific exponential zoom widths instead of physical-width tags. On the Trail map, normal paved strokes are about 3.52/6 px at zoom 16/18, unpaved about 4.57/6 px; high contrast uses 4/10 px and 5/10 px respectively. Road cycling does not inflate those strokes. The solid white ground casing has its own 6/12 px curve (7/16 px in high contrast), fading in from zoom 13 to 14. Bridges have separate deck/stroke widths; tunnels are narrower and faded, with long dashes and no added white casing. Blue is `#3148d8`, darkened to `#001bc7` in high contrast except in tunnels. Surface dashes are long for paved, short for unpaved and intermediate for unknown; tunnels use the long pattern. Explicitly bike-allowed footways/pedestrian streets use the cycling style; MTB-graded footways retain difficulty. Other pedestrian ways do not imply a prohibition. Signed routes stay separate. Other basemap appearances retain their existing rules.

Cycling restriction side marks focus on paths and forest tracks, including cycling paths, excluding ordinary motor-road classes and footways. They use red `#ff003c`, 2/3/6 px strokes at zoom 14/16/18 (3/6/9 px in high contrast), with short closely spaced dashes. Their left-side offset follows zoom and detailed trail width class, with a usual-width fallback for vector tiles. Tunnel marks fade to 30%; general/private-access marks remain separate. Missing permission is not a prohibition.

Downhill MTB difficulty has turquoise S0− (`--mtb-s0-minus`) for very easy paths and neon-green S0 (`--mtb-s0`); S1–S6 retain the indexed scale's colors. The map, cards, keys and route/profile use the same class and words. Uphill, hiking, speed and smoothness keep the original indexed palette, including its darker green S0.

### Primary
- **Accent (forest green)**: links, the chosen chip's border, the chosen switch's icon, ticks and the active state of map buttons. In dark mode a lighter green, so it still reads on dark surfaces.
- **Focus (green)**: the focus ring, on every control: the accent's green in light mode and a lighter one in dark.
- **Accent-bg (forest green)**: the background behind white text and icons, such as the main action (`btn primary`), **Plan**, the sport pill and pressed buttons. Light mode uses the accent itself; dark mode a darker green, because white on the lighter dark-mode accent is only 2.7:1, and on this one 5.3:1 (#239).
- **Accent-2 (lighter green)** and **Accent-soft (pale green tint)**: hover and the soft fill behind a chosen item.

### Secondary
- **Route (magenta)**: the route line on the map and its glow. It is the one strong colour on the screen and is kept for the route. A navigation magenta, apart from every red the map draws (the Trail map's S3 trails and no-bike ticks, LIPAS's hiking routes), and from them with colour blindness too (#547).
- **Route-text (a darker magenta, a pink in dark mode)**: words in the route's colour, such as the Weather button's time ahead and the timeline's ⚑, at 4.5:1 on either glass over any map (#559). The route's own colour is under that on the glass.
- **The route's legs**: over water deep blue (`--route-water`), with the same glow, and on the light maps a dark edge in place of the white one, which the sea's pale blue loses (#611); the map's key says so ("Over water"); a straight line darker magenta and dashed (`--route-line`); a leg being routed grey and dotted (`--route-loading`). They sit on the map, so they're the same in both themes.
- **Colour by** in the planner can recolour the route line on the map (with its **On map** switch) by grade, uphill difficulty, surface, kind of way, trail width, wind, temperature or rain; with any of them the plain magenta is replaced along the whole line. Each scale's colours are values too (`--diff-s0` … `--diff-s6`, `--grade-…`, `--surf-…`, `--sport-…`, `--way-…`, `--trail-width-…`, `--trail-vis-…`, `--temp-…`, `--rain-…`, `--wind-…`), while its words and order stay in its table in `js/config.js`, `js/chart.js` or `js/weather.js`. The weather map's own colours are still in `js/wxmap.js`. How the scales read, for colour blindness too, is in the design-system plan's §2.
- **In the JavaScript**, the route's colours, the warning colours and the scales come from the CSS: `fromCss()` in `js/palette.js` reads them at start and again when the theme changes, and the map and the planner draw with them again.
- **You (blue)**: the location dot and its accuracy circle (`--you-soft`). Blue is kept for "where you are" and for water.

### Tertiary
- **Good (green), Ok (amber), Warn (orange), Bad (red)**: states and warnings, such as Heads-up rows, difficulty and conditions, as lines, bars, dots and icons. Never the only way something is said: each comes with words or an icon. Bad is Danger's red, so danger has one red. Each has a lighter version in dark mode, so red words such as Remove and Delete read there too (#370).
- **Good-text, Ok-text, Warn-text, Bad-text**: the same four for words and pills, at least 6.2:1 in both themes: the condition pills, a warning on a layer's tile, and Check's count (warn-text behind it, the surface's colour on it, so it reads in both themes).
- **Ink (black)**: words on one of the map's or a scale's colours, where white doesn't read on it: a climb's badge, a loop's letter. `darkWords()` in `js/palette.js` picks black or white, whichever reads, at 4.58:1 or more. The same in both themes, since it sits on the colour.
- **Wx-text (blue)**: the weather's blue, for words: an active weather layer's chip in Layers. Lighter in dark mode.
- **Danger (red), Look (amber), Unk (blue-grey)**, each with a soft version for the box behind it: Check's three groups (25d), Read before you go, Worth a look and Not checked. Each has its shape too: a triangle, an eye, a dashed "?". At least 5.4:1 on its box and on the page, in both themes. Danger's box is solid in dark mode too, so it doesn't take the glass's tint. 25d's Journey C is the first to use them.

### Neutral
- **Glass-read (white at 72 %; near-black at 73 % in dark mode)**: the reading glass, for what you read over the map: the top bar with ⚙, the zoom hint, the planner, the timeline, the toolbox and the desktop's toolbar, the layer drawer, the panels, the card at a point on a desktop (at the point, or beside the panel) and a track's card.
- **Glass-control (white at 84 %; near-black at 84 %)**: the whiter glass of the map's buttons: the compass, Locate, zoom, 3D, Weather, Layers when open and the 3D view's controls, so they read as one group and stay close to one tint over a busy map.
- **Muted-glass, accent-glass and bad-glass**: a darker grey, green and red on the glass (lighter in dark mode), so words keep 4.5:1 and icons 3:1 over the Trail map, Maastokartta and the aerial photos (`tools/smoke.py glass`). Every glass surface sets its grey, green and red to these, so its lines, borders and rings take them too; those aren't measured over the map yet.
- **Surface-solid (white)**: menus, popups, dialogs, inputs and buttons that need an opaque background, and every glass when the person asks for more contrast or less transparency.
- **Surface-2 (pale grey-green)**: hover on rows and buttons, and recessed areas.
- **Text (near-black green)** and **Muted (grey-green)**: body text and secondary text, such as hints, units and section labels.
- **Line (text at 14 %)**: borders and dividers.
- **For more contrast**: **Muted-hc**, a grey closer to the text; **Line-hc**, lines at 35 %; and the rim, **Hc-border** outside and **Hc-inner** inside, 3:1 against any map.
- **Tile-rim**: the Layers button's light inner rim, over its picture.
- **Grip (grey)**: every grip and handle, at least 3:1 on its surface. Today the grips are still a paler grey (#382's controls and touch PRs).
- **Page (pale grey-green)**: the page behind the map, seen only before the map draws.
- **Chart ink and chart grid**: the profile's words and lines, drawn on its canvas.
- The map's own colours (land, water, parks, labels) are in `js/style.js`, one palette per map style. The sports' own line colours are `--sport-…` (`SPORT_COLORS` in `js/config.js`).

### Named Rules
**The Map Holds the Colour Rule.** Interface surfaces stay neutral. Magenta belongs to the route, red to danger, blue to water and to where you are; the accent marks what's chosen or the one main action, nothing decorative. **Green is for what's chosen** (decided 2026-11-16 in #382, after #869's "green means you can tap it"): a chosen chip or segment, the one main action, and a fold's or a way back's words, never plain information. A value you pick is in the text's colour with a grey ▾, as any key's words are. **Plan** is green, like every other main action. Today one thing breaks it, decided on 2026-10-22 (#382): **Colour by**'s wind scale draws headwinds in red, and goes orange to blue in the map and scales PR.

**The Never by Colour Alone Rule.** Every colour that means something (a warning, a difficulty, a chosen state) is also said in words, a tick, an icon or a shape (PRODUCT.md, WCAG 2.2 AA).

## Typography

**Typefaces** (Mine 9, #382), served by Polku from `fonts/` and kept by the service worker, both under the SIL Open Font License:
- **Bricolage Grotesque** (`--face-display`) for numbers and headings: a card's and a panel's title, the route's name, a section's heading, the numbers.
- **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next** (`--face-text`) for words: everything else.
- **One zero:** every figure is Bricolage's, in words too, so every zero looks the same (`Polku Figures`, Bricolage's digits by `unicode-range`, first in `--face-text`).
- Until the files arrive the device's font shows (`font-display: swap`), sized to theirs so lines don't jump. The profile's words on its canvas use the same families. The map's own labels are the map's (phase 20).

**Character:** two faces, numbers and headings apart from words; bold only for numbers, headings and the main action.

### Hierarchy

One value for each role, on `:root`, in `rem`, so the browser's text size enlarges it. Each has two sizes by the pointer, never the window's width: by touch (`pointer: coarse`) and with a mouse, denser, since a desktop is used up close. [docs/kit.html](docs/kit.html) draws them, both columns, light and dark. The sizes are tuned there.

| Role | Value | By touch | With a mouse | Face, weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A card's title | `--font-card` | 22 | 17 | Bricolage 700 |
| The route's name | `--font-name` | 19 | 16 | Bricolage 700 |
| A section's heading | `--font-heading` | 17 | 14 | Bricolage 700 |
| A number | `--font-number` | 20 | 16 | Bricolage 700 |
| A number's unit | `--font-unit` | 14 | 12 | Atkinson 400 |
| A row's name, a key's words | `--font-row` | 15 | 13 | Atkinson 500, a key 600 |
| A row's line, a number's word | `--font-line` | 13 | 12 | Atkinson 400 |
| What you type | `--font-field` | 16 | 13 | Atkinson 400 (16 by touch, so iOS doesn't zoom) |
| A number read while moving | `--font-glance` | 34 | 34 | Bricolage 700 |
| Small on purpose | `--font-small` | 11 | 11 | Atkinson 400 |

The weights are `--weight-regular` (400), `--weight-row` (500), `--weight-key` (600) and `--weight-strong` (700).

The planner and the cards take their sizes, weights and corners from these values, each by what its text does: a card's title `--font-card`, a row's name `--font-row`, the line under it `--font-line`, a number `--font-number`. A desktop's card at its spot (`.card-float`) takes the same list as everything else used with a mouse; only its mark is smaller. Not on the list yet: Settings, Layers, the menus, the toolbar and the rest still type their sizes in px (`python3 tools/smoke.py tokens` counts them, and the count only falls), and `--font-body` and `--weight-name` (600) wait for them. A few of the planner's and the cards' still do too: a waypoint's figure and a loop's letter, which are the map's own markers' text, and the charts' bars and swatches, which get one look with the kit's charts. The plain row (`row`) and `way-chips`' chips are shared with Settings and move with it.

### Named Rules
**The Tabular Numbers Rule.** Numbers that change or line up (times, distances, the timeline) use tabular figures, so they don't jump as they update.

**The Room for Finnish Rule.** Names, headings and labels wrap rather than run out of their box (`overflow-wrap: anywhere`), because Finnish and Swedish are coming (phase 18) and their words are long. A button's words wrap inside it: buttons have a least height, never a fixed one (#373). On a narrow card (under about 220 px, as at 200 %), a row's value goes under its name (#384).

**The Small on Purpose Rule.** Polku is a map, and some text is small so the map has the room: the scale bar, the credits, the map's own labels, chart axes, a word under an icon, a number inside a mark. Those take `--font-small` (11 px), and some still type 10 px until the migration. Text you read to decide or act (names, values, actions, warnings, a row's line) is 12 px or more. Small or not, text passes 4.5:1 and grows with the browser's text size.

## Layout

The map is fixed behind everything (`#map`, inset 0). Over it:
- **The top bar** sits 12 px from the top and left edges, up to 730 px wide. On a desktop 900 px wide or more it is 682 px (the logo, the search and the sport), and ⚙ stands alone 12 px from the top and right edges, over the panel it opens.
- **The map's buttons** stand at the right edge.
- **The desktop's toolbar** floats at the map's foot, rounded at every corner and 12 px from the planner (above it with the planner at the bottom). **Done** and, in the route's view, **Edit** are its first button, at the planner's end, the end that doesn't move.
- **The planner** is a side panel on a desktop and a sheet from the bottom on a phone, with a peek, a middle and a full height. The layer drawer is a card beside the Layers button on a desktop, a sheet on a phone; Settings is a panel under ⚙ on a desktop and a sheet like the drawer on a phone.

The switch is at **720 px**: up to 720 px wide is the phone layout, 721 px and up the desktop one; there's a wider step at 900 px, and short screens (under 500 px or 760 px high) get a more compact sheet. Touch is decided by the pointer, not the width: `pointer: coarse` grows targets to 44 px, and hover styles apply only where `hover: hover`.

Spacing is a tight rhythm: 6 and 8 px between items in a row, 10 to 14 px of padding inside cards, 12 px from the screen's edges. Its values are 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16 and 24 px (`--space-2` … `--space-24`), for new work and for a control when it moves onto the values. Targets are 44 px by touch at any width, 8 px apart (#380), and 24 px or more with a mouse: `--target` is 32 px, and 44 px under `pointer: coarse`. A row is `--row-h` (50 px by touch, 32 px with a mouse) and a card's mark `--mark` (34 px; 28 px on a desktop's card). Space by role: a group's padding `--group-pad` (4 px), the gap between groups `--group-gap` (12 px), and `--words-in` (15 px), the inset that lines a group's words up with its heading. A target is the area that answers a tap, not the size it looks: a control can be small to look at, with its touch area grown around it. Today many controls are still smaller (#235).

## Elevation & Depth

Depth comes from floating over the map: what floats is glass with one soft shadow, and the map moves under it. The reading glass blurs what's under it by 32 px and lifts its colours by 5 % (`--glass-read-filter`: `blur(32px) saturate(105%)`), so a sheet stays one quiet tone over any map and its trails still show; the map's buttons blur by 24 px (`--glass-control-filter`: `blur(24px) saturate(105%)`). One shadow token (`--shadow`) carries most of what floats; nothing stacks shadows to make a hierarchy. There are four shadow values; today about 38 other shadows are still typed in place in `css/app.css`, and they move onto the four when they're next touched (#382).

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Marker** (`--shadow-marker`: `0 1px 4px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.35)`, the same in both themes): a marker on the map.
- **Float** (`--shadow`: `0 8px 28px rgba(16, 24, 20, 0.18), 0 1px 3px rgba(16, 24, 20, 0.1)`; `0 8px 28px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.35), 0 1px 3px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.25)` in dark mode): every card, sheet, menu and map button over the map.
- **Edge** (`--edge`: `inset 0 0 0 1px` white at 70 %, `inset 0 1px 0` white at 95 % and `0 0 0 1px rgba(16, 24, 20, 0.26)`; in dark mode white at 34 % and 42 % inside and black at 70 % outside): every glass surface's two-tone edge, light inside and soft outside, beside the float shadow. In more contrast it's the 3:1 rim.
- **Sheet edge** (`--shadow-sheet`: `0 -6px 28px rgba(16, 24, 20, 0.2)`; black at 0.5 in dark mode): the phone's bottom sheet and the panels' edge, casting upwards.
- **Dialog** (`--shadow-dialog`: `0 24px 60px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.35)`, the same in both themes): a dialog over everything.
- **Ring** (`inset 0 0 0 2px` in the accent or the route colour): a chosen or open state, added to the float shadow.

### Named Rules
**The One Float Rule.** Everything over the map uses the same `--shadow`. A chosen state adds a ring, never a bigger shadow.

**The Edge Rule.** Every glass surface has the edge with its shadow (`box-shadow: var(--shadow), var(--edge)`). A ring for a chosen or open state adds to it.

**The Clear Glass Rule.** When the person asks for reduced transparency (`prefers-reduced-transparency`) or more contrast (`prefers-contrast: more`, or Settings' **High contrast**), or picks Settings' **Fewer effects** (`data-glass="solid"`), every glass turns solid (`--surface-solid`, no blur). More contrast also takes the darker grey, heavier lines and the 3:1 rim (`data-contrast` on `<html>`, set as `data-theme` is).

## Shapes

Gently rounded, never sharp. The corners are values: `--radius-small` (6 px) for small things such as swatches and inputs, `--radius-inner` (7 px) for a control inside a track, `--radius-control` (10 px) for a control, `--radius` (14 px) for cards, panels and tiles, `--radius-sheet` (20 px) for the top of a phone's sheet, `--radius-pill` (999 px) for chips, the sport pill and switches, and `--radius-round` (50 %) for icon-only map buttons and dots. In the planner and the cards a key and a field are 10 px, a group's rows 10 px, and a note, a box and a card 14 px; the plain row shared with Settings is still 12 px; elsewhere buttons and rows are still 10 to 12 px until their area moves onto the values. The planner's sheet floats 8 px in from the screen's edges on a phone, with 20 px corners all round (`--radius-sheet`); the layer drawer's and the panels' sheets have 20 px corners at the top only. Borders are a single 1 px line in `--line`, used sparingly.

## Components

The controls, with their classes and where each comes from, are in **[docs/DEVELOPING.md, "Shared controls"](docs/DEVELOPING.md#shared-controls)**. That table is the one list: this file doesn't repeat it. A new screen uses those controls for the same jobs, a mock-up copies their CSS from `css/app.css`, and a new control is proposed to the user first and then joins that table.

On the glass:
- **The Layers button**: its map picture with a light inner rim (`--tile-rim`) and the glass's soft outer line; its word on a dark band that's 4.5:1 or more over a white picture.
- **An undo that can't undo** on the toolbox: the glass's grey at 70 %, readable and still dimmer than redo.
- **The logo on a phone**: the green mark alone, 44 px, with no pill round it. On a desktop its pill's corners are concentric with the mark's.

### One style for the planner and the cards

Decided 2026-11-15 and 2026-11-16 (#382, from #869's rule for controls, #690's cards and round 5's look), and built a small PR at a time; what isn't built yet says so. The mock-up is [mockups/design-system-grouped.html](docs/plans/mockups/design-system-grouped.html), version **Mine 8**. It's a sketch: where a detail looks better otherwise in the app (dark mode, a padding, a colour), the app decides. Polku is read outdoors on a phone, in sun and with one thumb, so what you read is big enough and what you can press is plain to see.

**Sizes, by the pointer, never the window's width** (decided 2026-11-16). By touch XS 28, S 32, M 40, L 44 and XL 52 px, and every control's target is 44 px however small it draws. With a mouse they're denser: S 24, M 28, L 32 and XL 40 px. They're `--size-s`, `--size-m`, `--target` (L) and `--size-xl`; XS gets its value with the first control that uses it. The words: see **Type and space** below.

**The numbers' box.** A route's or a card's numbers sit in one white box, as round 5 has them, a thin line between them. When they don't fit one line they wrap to a second rather than shrink. Built for the planner (2026-11-16): the box on the surface with a thin `--line` edge and lines between main's three numbers, wrapping where they don't fit (at 200 %, without the lines).

**The planner's head** (built 2026-11-16; a desktop's planner keys on a line of their own over it). The route's name as its title, its sports in their colours and its changeovers under it on a route of more than one sport, the warnings beside it (a red ball with its count, or **?** in a ring while only what wasn't checked is left, its words in its name; a tap opens Check at the first), then the numbers' box, a thin line between the numbers. When and the weather follow in a group of their own, a line between them, over the profile; the key facts go under the profile as round keys (M, the key's face and edge, a sport's in its bubble). The ball's place beside the name is kept whether or not Check has answered, so the name never re-wraps when the answer comes late (#741). The peek is the title, the numbers and the chart.

**Headings.** Every section under the head has one, inside its group at the group's top, its tools at its end (they go under it where its words need the room): **Route profile** (its size ⌃, an icon key, M; **Conditions** on the water), **Route** (Show all waypoints and the list's settings) and **Route style and your speed** (built 2026-11-16, `pl-h`). When and the weather have none: they follow the numbers. A card's are its own: At this waypoint, Look out for, Profile. At a phone's peek and half the profile has no heading and no group, and When follows the chart, so the chart doesn't move as the sheet does (#742).

**Glass and groups.** The sheet is see-through over the map (the reading glass with a blur); each section is a group on it, so the groups read as one sheet's parts and the map still shows around them. Built 2026-11-16 as one class, `pl-group`: Mine 8's white at 74 % (`--group`, the surface's colour, so dark mode has its own), no edge on the glass and a `--line` edge where the glass is solid (Fewer effects, High contrast, reduced transparency: `--group-edge`), 14 px corners, 4 px round what it holds and its rows' corners nesting at 10 px, so a group's words start 15 px in (`--words-in`); 12 px between groups (`--group-gap`).

**Rows that scroll sideways.** Where a row of chips doesn't fit (the key facts, the timeline's layers), it scrolls sideways rather than wrapping: no bar, a fade at the edge where there's more, and with a mouse an arrow there and a drag (`sideways()` in `js/ui.js`). Tab brings a chip out from under the fade and the arrow, and an arrow takes clicks for a double-click's interval (500 ms) after its last click, so clicking it again and again at the row's end doesn't open the chip under it once it has faded. A scroll by a wheel, a trackpad or a drag leaves no arrow behind. Nothing in it is cut. Actions never scroll away: a row of keys that does something (the list's Reorder, Reverse, Back to start, Clear route) wraps, so every action stays in sight.

**A quiet scrollbar.** The planner, the cards and the panels have a thin scrollbar that shows only while they scroll and under a mouse.

**A sport in its bubble.** A sport's icon in a filled circle of its colour (white in light mode, black on dark mode's lighter colours): a part's heading in Route's list (on the list's line, 32 px, the user's choice of 2026-11-16 over Mine 8's ball at the list's left), Route style's rows and the key facts.

**Type and space** (2026-11-16, from the user's review: a desktop's planner looked outsized, and every gap was alike, so nothing read as grouped). The planner takes the type list (**Typography**): the route's name `--font-name`, a section's heading `--font-heading`, a number `--font-number`, a row's or a key's words `--font-row` and a line under them `--font-line`; with a mouse its rows are `--row-h`, 32 px. Space says what belongs together: a section is one group, its heading inside it; 8 px between things inside a group, 12 px between groups; When and the weather sit 12 px under the numbers, part of the head.

**Controls by job:**
1. **The one main action:** filled green (`--accent-bg`, white words), the whole width, at most one in a panel. The planner, the toolbar and each card are panels of their own: a stretch's **Edit this stretch**, a spot's **Add a waypoint here**.
2. **Does something now:** a key: a flat white face, a 1 px edge in `--key-edge` (as clear as High contrast's lines, so it reads as a key in the sun), 10 px corners and an icon before its words, L tall. Built for every `btn` and a card's actions; the main action has the same corners. The drawing toolbar keeps its own keys until #1042. Not built yet: a card's keys in one row that scrolls sideways, as Google Maps' are, so none hides at the card's end, with removing last, in red.
3. **Bare icons,** from a fixed few: ⌄ ⌃ ✕ ⋯ ↶ ↷ ⚙, the list's settings and full screen, L: the sheet's top row and a panel's ✕.

Beside those:
- **A value you pick** (Leave now ▾, Colour by ▾): the value in the text's colour on a round key with the key's face and edge, and a grey ▾, never a pale green field. Colour by is M; When stays L, the planner's first value.
- **A choice** (a chip): a round key with the key's face and edge, its words in the text's colour, M where a chip has no size of its own; a chosen one has a green tint and a 2 px green edge.
- **A row that opens something** (Route style ›, Your speed ›, Sport from here ›, the list's rows): 56 px, the whole row a target, its value green before ›, a line between rows.
- **A switch** for on and off; **segments** for one of a few: M buttons on a grey track 3 px round them with the key's edge, corners 10 px outside and 7 inside, the chosen one green as a chosen chip is (built 2026-11-16). A card's icon segments (Pass by · Stop · Night) stay L, as every key on a card is; **the key facts** as round keys; **a stepper** as − and + keys around the value.
- **Not pressable:** numbers and their words have no edge, no › and no ▾.
- **Lines that stay:** a row keeps its line when it guides (To the next change of sport, or the end) or holds data (a time, what stays where, what failed). Filler goes. A key has no line: with a mouse its tooltip says more.
- **Before a button:** each action is asked whether it needs one. On the profile goes from a point's card, whose own profile is right there.

### A card's places

Every card has the same places, in this order. A place it has nothing for is left out; the order never changes:
1. **The top row:** ⌄ at the left, ⋯ and ✕ at the right.
2. **‹ Route,** on its own line, where the card was opened from the route.
3. **The title:** the map's own mark and the name.
4. **The numbers:** one aligned row with thin lines between, the most important first; warnings a red ball with their count, last.
5. **The actions:** the main one, whole width, then the row of keys.
6. **The sections:** what the card is for first (At this waypoint, Look out for), then its profile.
7. **The foot:** stepping along the route, ‹ and › around the strip of the route's points and stretches (or "3 of 9"), as the warning card's ‹ 1 of 2 ›. It stays at the card's foot while the card scrolls.

The planner keeps its own order: its head, then its four sections.

**Settings and Layers:** a switch's explanation is behind an ⓘ right after its name. The ⓘ is a button in the switch's label, so a press on it doesn't flip the switch, and the switch is named by its name alone. A note that keeps you safe stays as words, as the depth layers', Sea ice's and the ski tracks' do, and so does a note that holds data, shows the way into an empty list or promises something about your data.

**Check** is a view of its own, in place of #424's tabs. Check's row leads Route's view with the warnings' count, ‹ Route goes back, and the warnings ball beside the route's name opens it at the first one.

What every control shares:
- **Focus:** one 2 px outline in `--focus`, 2 px out, set once for every button, link, field and focusable element (`:where(…):focus-visible`); inside a row of a list or a menu's item (#385).
- **Hover:** `--surface-2` behind, only with a mouse. Today 38 of 57 hover rules also apply on touch screens, where they stay after a tap (#208), and the hover looks vary (`--surface-2`, `--surface-solid`, `--accent-soft`, `--accent-bg`).
- **Pressed:** buttons, chips, tiles and switches, the map's buttons and the top bar's too, shrink to 97 % for `--t-press` while held, with no grey flash; rows keep their grey (`--surface-2`). A key a mouse drag starts on, in a row that scrolls sideways, lets go of its press. With reduced motion, nothing moves.
- **Chosen:** never colour alone (#356). A chip: a green tint, a 2 px green border, black words. A switch: white, black words, a green icon or tick. A tile: a green border, icon and tick, black words. A row: a green tint and a tick. A switch that's off has a `--muted` edge, at least 3:1.
- **Motion:** see [Motion](#motion) below: the timings, the curves and when things move.

## Motion

Polku's motion follows Emil Kowalski's design engineering ([Great animations](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations), [7 practical animation tips](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips)) and the skills built on it: `/animate` decides each motion and `/review-animations` checks it. The aim is motion nearly everywhere it helps: every press answers, and every change that means something moves. What people do many times a minute stays instant. Where a rule isn't yet true everywhere, #540's Motion 1, 2 and 3 make it true.

- **Timings:** `--t-press` (120 ms) for a press and hover, `--t-open` (180 ms) for cards and menus and `--t-sheet` (260 ms) for the sheet and the panel; closing takes the next shorter one. Interface motion stays under 300 ms. Today hover and press take 120 to 150 ms in places, typed in place.
- **Curves:** entering and leaving ease out on `--ease-out` (`cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`), and the sheet on `--ease-drawer` (`cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)`); never ease-in. What moves with a finger uses the sheet's spring (`js/sheet.js`).
- **Whether it moves:** never for an action started from the keyboard. What people see tens or hundreds of times a day (typing, undo, adding points, scrubbing) gets no motion, and hover effects come only with a mouse (`@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`). First paint and page load never animate, nor does a change of light, dark or contrast: the new look is drawn everywhere at once.
- **Press:** buttons, chips, tiles and switches shrink to 97 % while held (Pressed, under Components).
- **Entering:** nothing enters from `scale(0)`: from 0.9 or more (tooltips 0.97), with opacity. Menus, popovers and tooltips grow from their trigger (`transform-origin` at the trigger; dialogs stay centred) and leave the way they came, faster; a menu that opens in the place of one still leaving comes at once, and one with no room beside its trigger, a sheet of its own, only fades.
- **Tooltips:** a delay before the first one; once one is open, the next opens at once, with no animation.
- **Interrupting:** a move starts from where things are and can be caught mid-way: CSS transitions rather than keyframes.
- **Performance:** interface elements animate `transform` and `opacity` only (`filter: blur()` where needed), with CSS or WAAPI rather than `requestAnimationFrame`, so they stay smooth while the main thread is busy. The map's canvas effects (wind, rain, snow) draw each frame by nature. The one exception is a desktop card folding to its title row, which animates its height (120 ms to fold, 180 ms to open, `roll()` in `js/card.js`): `clip-path` would cut its shadow while it moves.
- **A crossfade** between two states that still feels off gets 2 px of blur.
- **The map:** colour and opacity changes use MapLibre's paint transitions; camera moves share one set of durations; no tweening of geometry.
- **Rebuilt content:** where only a state changes, the control shows its change through `settleControls()` (`js/ui.js`); nothing re-animates on a rebuild the person didn't cause. What opens in a rebuilt panel (a group's rows, a sport's speeds, a stretch's waypoints) fades in only on the tap that opened it.
- **What changes place** fades in where it lands, in `--t-open` on `--ease-out`: on a phone, When and the profile as the planner goes between half and full (full puts When over the profile, the peek and half under it), and Check's view and Route style's steps on a press. It's opacity only, so reduced motion keeps it; from the keyboard they change at once.
- **Outdoors and battery:** nothing loops while idle unless it says something (busy), and it stops when done. A busy bar waits 300 ms before it shows, so a quick answer shows nothing, and it is never under something hidden.
- **Reduced motion:** opacity only: things fade in place instead of sliding, pulses stop, the map's camera moves jump, and the planner's list scrolls at once. A chosen look's colours still fade, since nothing moves, and nothing shrinks, grows or turns. A busy bar (routing) stays in place and pulses in opacity, since it says something.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** use the CSS variables (`var(--accent)`, `var(--glass-read)`, `var(--shadow)`), never their hex values, so dark mode follows. In the JavaScript, a colour that means something comes from the CSS through `fromCss()` in `js/palette.js`.
- **Do** keep targets at least 44 px for touch, and say every meaning in words as well as colour.
- **Do** put white text on `--accent-bg`, not on `--accent` (#239).
- **Do** reuse a control from DEVELOPING's "Shared controls" for a job it already does.

### Don't:
- **Don't** use red for anything but danger, magenta for anything but the route, or blue for anything but water and where you are.
- **Don't** add a second shadow, a heavier shadow or a new radius to make something stand out; use the ring, the accent or the words.
- **Don't** make a copy of an existing control under a new class name.
- **Don't** cover the map with anything the moment doesn't need.
